WoW Forever guide

Which ruleset?

Forever has no realm list. You choose a ruleset when you make a character, and it decides who you can group with, whether other players can attack you, and what a death costs. What each of the five does, how they connect, and a picker if you are undecided.

If you only read one thing

The short version

Pick the ruleset your friends are on. You can only group with players on your own ruleset, and moving means starting a new character.

THOMAS, DLONEM

If you have no group yet, Normal is the safe default: nobody can attack you unless you both agree to it, and you can play both factions. PvP is for people who want the world to be dangerous. Roleplaying is for people who want to stay in character. Roleplay-PvP, announced on 22 October, is for people who want both. Hardcore is one life, and it opens after release.

I play Normal, on the Alliance, in North America. When I want a fight, I turn PvP on myself; how that works.

What each one is

The five rulesets

The descriptions are Blizzard's. The rest is what each one means in practice.

The default

Normal

Blizzard calls it the baseline World of Warcraft experience. Player-versus-player combat needs both players to agree to it.

Good for
Questing in peace, playing both factions, most guilds.
Watch for
Nothing in particular. Battlegrounds are still there when you want a fight.
Danger on the road

PvP

For players who want every trip into contested territory to carry more danger and open-world conflict.

Good for
World PvP, faction rivalry, a server that feels like a war.
Watch for
One faction per account on this ruleset, and getting attacked while you quest.
Stay in character

Roleplaying

For players who want to immerse themselves in the fantasy of Azeroth and find others who do.

Good for
Roleplay guilds, events, a slower pace.
Watch for
Blizzard has not published separate rules for it, so treat it as a community choice.
New for release

Roleplay-PvP

Roleplaying with open-world fighting between the factions. Blizzard first said it had concerns about adding it, then announced on 22 October 2026 that it would be there from 4 November.

Good for
Roleplayers who want the other faction to be a real threat on the road.
Watch for
Its rules are not published yet: whether it locks an account to one faction as PvP does, and how flagging works.
One life

Hardcore

For players who want the greatest challenge, where death has lasting consequences. It opens after release.

Good for
Players who want every pull to matter.
Watch for
A sealed ruleset: nothing from your other characters comes in.

The map

How they connect

Each ruleset is its own large community. You can group, run dungeons and raid only inside your own, and Horde and Alliance cannot group together on any of them. Battlegrounds are the one place Normal, PvP and Roleplaying players can meet; Hardcore stays out.

Collections and Legacy Points are account-wide. Progress from Hardcore can carry out to the other rulesets, but nothing from them flows into Hardcore.

Contested ground

The PvP ruleset

On PvP you are flagged for combat automatically when you enter a contested or enemy zone, so the other faction can attack you there without asking. Icy Veins' PvP overview says zones above level 15 count as contested; Blizzard has not published that threshold itself.

On Normal you only fight other players if you turn PvP on yourself. Type /pvp in chat, or right-click your portrait and turn PvP on. From then on you can attack flagged players of the other faction, and they can attack you. That is how I play world PvP: on Normal, when I feel like it. A few add-ons make that easier, from a shared map of enemy sightings to one that keeps you flagged once you swear to it; they are on the add-ons page.

Two rules to know before you commit:

  • One faction per account. Once you make an Alliance character on PvP, you cannot make a Horde one there, and the other way round.
  • Faction balance. Blizzard says it will add systems to keep Horde and Alliance balanced on PvP, as it did in Season of Discovery, which could temporarily limit making characters for one faction.

Honor, ranks and the PvP gear sets come from battlegrounds, player kills and PvP quests. Blizzard's PvP progression post has the rank rewards.

One life

The Hardcore ruleset

Hardcore opens after release, and Blizzard has not given a date. It is a closed community: you can't group with players on other rulesets, and its characters don't join the mixed battleground queues.

The rule that sets it apart is what happens at the end. When a Hardcore character dies, it is not deleted. It can transfer to another ruleset, PvP included, and keep playing.

I don't plan to play Hardcore. If you do, be careful, and never walk away from your computer while your character is somewhere dangerous.

Whichever you pick

Rules for all five

  • No realm list. You choose a ruleset when you make a character, and the whole region shares it.
  • Same ruleset, same faction. You can only group with players on your ruleset and your faction.
  • No free switching. To play on another ruleset you make a new character there.
  • Names are unique in your region, across every ruleset. How names work.
  • Language. Set a preferred language in Game Menu, Options, Languages and Social, Preferred Online Language. It is account-wide and can only be changed once every 24 hours.
Quick answers

Questions

What are the rulesets in WoW Forever?

Normal, PvP, Roleplaying, Roleplay-PvP and Hardcore. Forever has no realm list; you choose a ruleset when you make a character instead. Blizzard announced Roleplay-PvP on 22 October 2026, and said Hardcore would open after the other rulesets.

What is the Roleplay-PvP ruleset?

A ruleset for roleplayers who also want open-world fighting between the factions. Blizzard first said it had concerns about adding one, then announced on 22 October 2026 that it would be there from 4 November. Blizzard has not published its rules yet, such as whether it locks an account to one faction the way PvP does.

Can I fight other players on Normal?

Yes, if you choose to. Type /pvp in chat, or right-click your portrait and turn PvP on. You can then attack, and be attacked by, players of the other faction who are flagged too. Battlegrounds are open to Normal characters.

Can I group with friends on a different ruleset?

No. You can group, run dungeons and raid only with players on your own ruleset. Battleground queues can include players from other rulesets, except Hardcore.

Can I change ruleset later?

Not freely. To play on a different ruleset you make a new character there. The one exception Blizzard has described is a Hardcore character that dies, which can move to another ruleset.

Can I play both factions on the PvP ruleset?

No. On PvP an account can only make characters for one faction. Normal, Roleplaying and Hardcore allow both. Blizzard has not said yet which rule Roleplay-PvP follows.

When is Hardcore available?

After release. Blizzard has not given a date, and it is not part of the 4 November release.

What happens when a Hardcore character dies?

Its Hardcore run is over, but it is not deleted: it can transfer to another ruleset, PvP included, and keep playing there.

Does the ruleset change which names I can take?

No. A full name is unique within your region across every ruleset.

Which ruleset do you play?

Normal, on the Alliance, in North America. That is where my guild, The Grey Company, is.
