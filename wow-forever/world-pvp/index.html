World PvP on Normal

Picking the Normal ruleset doesn't mean battlegrounds only. On Normal you decide when other players can attack you: turn PvP on and the open world is a war zone again, with the fights you went looking for and none you didn't. How flagging works, how to find the other faction, and the two add-ons that turn it into a campaign.

If you only read one thing

The short version

The only real difference between Normal and PvP is consent. On Normal, everyone you fight chose to be there.

THOMAS, DLONEM

On Normal, type /pvp and you can attack flagged players of the other faction, and they can attack you. Type it again to switch it off; the flag drops after five minutes without a fight. Put it on a macro and you can flag up the moment you see something worth fighting.

Two add-ons make it more than wandering around hoping. War Effort shares every flagged enemy group its players see, so you know where the fights are. Overlord Forever gives those fights something to win: capture points on seven fronts, guild keeps and a weekly war. Keep enemy nameplates on (Ctrl+V); both of them see the enemy through nameplates.

The one rule nobody tells you: on Normal, War Effort only shares what you see while you are flagged. You still see everyone else's sightings either way.

Same world, different rules

Normal or PvP: it's about consent

Forever has no realm list. You pick a ruleset when you make a character, and for world PvP the difference comes down to who decides when a fight can start.

NormalPvP
Who can attack you in the open worldFlagged players of the other faction, and only while you are flagged tooAnyone of the other faction once you are in a contested or enemy zone
Who decidesYou: /pvp or your portraitThe zone flags you automatically
Characters on both factionsYesNo, one faction per account
BattlegroundsYesYes

Overlord ForeverIts own Normal campaign; flag needed to captureIts own PvP campaign; flag needed to capture

Icy Veins' PvP overview says zones above level 15 count as contested on the PvP ruleset; Blizzard has not published that threshold itself. The ruleset guide has all five rulesets side by side, including Roleplaying and Roleplay-PvP. War Effort treats Roleplaying like Normal and Roleplay-PvP like PvP.

Why I'd rather fight on Normal

On a PvP ruleset, everyone in a contested zone is in the fight whether they want to be or not, including the level 22 trying to finish a quest in Hillsbrad. On Normal, the people who show up chose to, so a handful of players can swing a front, and nobody's evening gets ruined by a fight they didn't ask for.

You also learn something from the players who aren't flagged. They can't hit you, and you can't hit them, but you can see them. Say seven of us are taking Eastvale Logging Camp on the Elwynn front, and one Horde player is standing by the road with PvP off. He isn't a threat yet; he's a warning. He is probably waiting for his friends, and when they get there he has to flag to join in. If he never does, we keep the point anyway.

On and off

How flagging works on Normal

  • Turning it on. Type /pvp in chat, or right-click your portrait and turn PvP on. You are flagged straight away.
  • Turning it off. Type /pvp again, or use the portrait menu. It isn't instant: the game's own text says you are unflagged after five minutes without PvP in friendly territory. Until then you can still be attacked, and War Effort keeps sharing what you see.
  • Attacking flags you. Hit a flagged player and you are flagged too, so anyone can join a fight you are already in. Nobody can start one with you while you are unflagged.
  • Healers, take note. In modern World of Warcraft, an unflagged player can't heal or buff a flagged one at all. Blizzard hasn't said how Forever handles it, so if your group flags up, flag with them.
  • Neutral towns. In Booty Bay, Gadgetzan, Ratchet and Everlook the guards attack whoever starts the fight, as they did in Classic.

A one-click flag

Typing /pvp works, but a button is faster when a fight turns up.

  1. Open the macro window. Type /macro, or press Escape and choose Macros.
  2. Make a new macro. Name it PvP, pick any icon, and give it the single line /pvp.
  3. Drag it to your action bar. One click flags you, and the next starts the five minutes until your flag drops.

If you'd rather stay flagged and never think about it, HonorBound turns your flag back on whenever something switches it off.

Find the fight

War Effort: every player is a scout

War Effort counts the flagged enemy players around you and shares them with your guild and with every War Effort player of your faction. Their sightings come back to you as groups on the world map and the minimap, with an alert when one comes close on your layer. The more people run it, the more of the world it sees, which is the whole reason I'm writing about it.

The rule nobody tells you

On Normal and Roleplaying, your sightings only go out while you are flagged. Unflagged, you still see everything the network sends you. What you see yourself stays on your own map, marked as seen by you only, and earns no scout points.

The add-on says so once a session: "On PvE and RP servers, your sightings are shared and earn scout points only while you are PvP-flagged." The author's reasoning is that an unflagged player can't be attacked, so there is no scouting without the risk. The War Effort button on your minimap reads Paused: turn PvP on while it is holding your sightings back. On PvP and Roleplay-PvP it shares all the time.

So if you want to feed the map and climb the scout ranks on Normal, travel flagged. If you only want to know where the fights are, you don't have to flag at all.

What counts as a sighting

  • Flagged enemies only. Players who haven't turned PvP on are never counted or shared.
  • Two or more. A group goes out from two flagged enemies. One player on their own is not reported.
  • Nameplates on. It spots enemies through their nameplates, so keep enemy nameplates switched on with Ctrl+V. Your target and mouseover add to a group but never start one.
  • Your level range. By default, enemies five or more levels below you don't count toward a group's size. You can set the gap anywhere from 1 to 8.
  • Open world only. Battlegrounds and dungeons are ignored, and so are crowds inside enemy and neutral towns and at instance entrances, unless you change it.

What you can do with it

  • Join their layer. Groups on other layers show their layer number. Shift-click one on the map to ask one of the players who reported it to invite you, which is how you reach their layer. The add-on never invites or accepts anything on its own; every step is a click.
  • Kill on sight. Right-click an enemy player's portrait to add them to your list, and you can have it warn you whenever a group on your map has one of them in it. The list stays on your computer.
  • Call for help. Die next to an enemy group while you are in a party or raid, and a button under the death window tells your party, raid or guild what killed you and where: how many, and their levels.
  • Climb the ranks. Fourteen scout ranks, from Lookout to Eye of the King on the Alliance side, with daily contracts, streaks and a guild leaderboard.
  • Pick how much you see. On first login it asks: full radar, radar without the map, radar without the map or minimap, or silent scout, which shares what you see and shows you nothing.

What it shares, and what stops fake reports

A sighting carries where you were standing, your layer, and the enemies' count, classes and levels. Enemy names are never shared or shown. Each group's tooltip lists up to three players who reported it, so you can see who saw it.

Anyone could send bad data from an edited copy, so the add-on doesn't take reports on trust. A big group seen by only one player is marked unconfirmed, with a question mark after its size. Reports with impossible counts, or from a reporter who jumps across the map, are dropped. If you stand where a report was and see nobody, it comes off your map. /we ignore <name> drops everything one player sends you.

Give the fight a reason

Overlord Forever: a war with a scoreboard

Overlord Forever turns the open world into a campaign. Each front is a chain of capture points between an Alliance end and a Horde end, and taking the enemy's end wins the front. Captures, kills and guild holdings feed a weekly war between the factions.

The names are the add-on's. Southshore against Tarren Mill is the one everybody remembers.

Capturing a point

  • PvP on, on foot, inside the circle. Capturing pauses while you are mounted, flying, in travel form, in a vehicle or on a flight path, stealthed or dead.
  • Two minutes for an ordinary point. The enemy's end takes eight, and retaking your own takes two. Step out and the progress falls back.
  • Numbers matter. Overlord counts the enemy players it sees in the circle. As many as you, or more, push the capture back, and faster the more they outnumber you. Bring friends.
  • Points open in order. A point opens once the points before it are yours, and several branches can be open at once.
  • Winning a front. Once every other point is yours, take the enemy's end. That wins the front and adds to your faction's weekly domination. The fallen end stays yours until its own faction takes it back, and a second win on the same front only counts six hours after the last. (Older descriptions mention a 15-minute truce after a win; the add-on dropped it on 20 October 2026.)

The rest of the war

  • Platinum mines. Standing in a mine's circle earns Overlord's own currency, platinum, with no flag needed. Spend 25 to make your next capture a minute shorter, or to make the enemy's next capture of one of your points a minute longer. Neither works on a front's two ends.
  • Guild keeps and outposts. Five keeps (Stonetalon Mountains, Wetlands, Badlands, the Barrens and Mulgore) take ten minutes to claim; outposts, one per front plus four elsewhere, take five. While your faction holds a front's outpost, the enemy needs 30 seconds longer on that front's ordinary points.
  • A featured front each day, and the war map and rankings reset every Tuesday at 08:00 UTC.
  • Call to arms, Generals and Nemeses. A call to arms reaches every Overlord player of your faction, once every four hours for the whole faction. A group leader can become the faction's General, shown on the map. The week's five best enemy killers wear a skull on their nameplate.
  • Rankings. Open-world honorable kills and captures count. Battlegrounds, arenas, dungeons and raids don't.
  • One campaign per ruleset. Normal, PvP, Roleplaying and Hardcore each keep their own war, so on Normal you are fighting other Normal players.

Better together

Using both

The two add-ons know about each other. With Overlord running, the scout points War Effort gives you are worth more when you earn them standing in one of Overlord's objectives:

Standing inScout point bonus
Front zone+10%
Guild outpost+25%
Front capital+35%
Guild keep+50%

Front points that neither faction can take at that moment don't count. War Effort's group list can also be filtered to Overlord objectives only, and its alerts can be set to fire only for groups at one.

How an evening can go

An example, on the Elwynn front:

  1. Log in and flag. One click on the macro. War Effort starts sharing what I see.
  2. Check the war. /ov show says which Elwynn points we hold and which one opens next.
  3. Check the map. Say War Effort shows a Horde group near Blackrock Advance, on my layer, heading west.
  4. Call it. Guild chat, or Overlord's call to arms if it is a big one. Seven of us head for Eastvale Logging Camp.
  5. Read the field. One Horde player waits by the road, unflagged. He can't touch us; he is telling us his group is coming.
  6. Hold the circle. Two minutes, on foot. If they flag up and outnumber us, the capture slides back and the fight starts.
Ten minutes, once

Setup checklist

  1. Choose your ruleset. Everything here is written for Normal; the ruleset guide covers the rest.
  2. Install both add-ons from CurseForge, picking WoW Forever as the game version. The add-ons guide has the folder and the common mistakes.
  3. Turn on enemy nameplates with Ctrl+V.
  4. Make the /pvp macro and put it on your bar.
  5. Answer War Effort's first question. Full radar if you want the map; silent scout if you only want to help.
  6. Read Overlord's guide with /ov guide. Three pages, and it covers things this page doesn't.
  7. Bring people. Both add-ons get better with every player in your guild who runs them.
Quick answers

Questions

Can you do world PvP on the Normal ruleset in WoW Forever?

Yes. Type /pvp in chat, or right-click your portrait and turn PvP on. You can then attack flagged players of the other faction anywhere in the open world, and they can attack you. Players who have not turned it on are out of reach, and you are out of theirs once your flag drops.

How do I turn PvP off again?

Type /pvp again, or use the portrait menu. The flag does not drop at once: the game's own text says you are unflagged after five minutes without PvP in friendly territory, and until then you can still be attacked.

Can a player who isn't flagged attack me?

Yes, if you are flagged. Attacking a flagged player flags the attacker too, so they join the fight the moment they hit you. If you are not flagged, nobody of the other faction can start a fight with you.

Does War Effort work on Normal?

Yes. You see every sighting your guild and faction send, flagged or not. Your own sightings are only shared, and only earn scout points, while you are flagged. Unflagged, they stay on your own map, marked as seen by you only.

Why aren't my War Effort sightings being shared?

On Normal or Roleplaying, the usual reason is that you are not flagged. The others: enemy nameplates are off (Ctrl+V), you only saw one enemy (it reports groups of two or more), the enemies were not flagged, or they were in a town, a dungeon or a battleground, which it leaves out.

Does Overlord Forever work on Normal?

Yes. Normal has its own campaign, separate from PvP, Roleplaying and Hardcore, so you are fighting other Normal players for the fronts. You need PvP on to capture a point.

Can someone fake a War Effort report?

Anyone can send bad data from an edited add-on, and War Effort is built with that in mind. Each group's tooltip names who reported it, a big group seen by only one player is marked unconfirmed, impossible counts and reporters who jump across the map are dropped, and if you stand where a report was and see nobody, it comes off your map. You can also ignore a player with /we ignore.

Do battleground kills count for Overlord?

No. Overlord's rankings count open-world honorable kills and captures. Battlegrounds, arenas, dungeons and raids are left out.

Do I need both add-ons?

No. Each works on its own. Together, War Effort shows where the other faction is, Overlord gives you a reason to go there, and War Effort's scout points are worth more when you earn them at an Overlord objective.

Is there a guild for this?

Mine. The Gray Company plays Alliance on Normal in North America, and I run both add-ons.
