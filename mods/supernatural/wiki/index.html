Start hereQuick answers#

The questions that come up most, grouped by topic. Tap a question to open its answer; each one links to the full section.

Vampires

How do I become a vampire?

Get a vampire's blood into you, then finish the change. A vampire or hybrid can use Offer Blood and then Turn Vampire on you, or you can use Ask to be Turned on a vampire you know. Falling in battle or dying a natural death with the blood in you also turns you. Nobody is born a vampire. Every route into vampirism.

Why did my county lose control after I fed?

A body leaves a mark on the county it was found in, and so does a feed somebody talks about. A drained body costs 5 county control; a feed that gets talked about costs 1. Two incidents inside a year open a trail, Something Stalks Here, and at the third the county's lord is told. Trails and what leaves them.

Werewolves

How do I turn someone into a werewolf, and can it be cured?

A werewolf uses Offer Gift or Force Gift. That gives the Infected Wound, which lasts one to three years and ends in a turning, a reprieve or death. The cure is the Abandon Lycanthropy decision, which needs the Supernatural: Embrace Decisions rule set to On. The rule is off by default. The bite, in full. The werewolf cure.

How do I get one of the six hides?

Be a werewolf or hybrid whose traits suit the hide, then get hold of it. Most start as relics in shrines at holy sites. Learn where one is at a Werewolf Gathering, then hold the county, besiege it, go there on pilgrimage, or use Send for a Hide. A hide that accepts you makes you its Champion. Where the hides are. Who each hide chooses.

Hunters

How does a hunter find me, and what can I do about it?

He follows the trail your kills leave on a county and tracks for three weeks. If his Tracking bar reaches 60 you are warned. At the door you choose an answer: fight, go to ground for two years, look him in the eye, send your progeny, let the wolf out, buy him off, or point him at a worse county. The door, from both sides.

How do I play a hunter?

Take the Hunter trait in the ruler designer, or use Ask to be trained as a Hunter on a hunter you know. You pick monsters or witches first, and training then takes 75 to 115 days. If it fails you become an apprentice and finish on a hunt with your trainer. The work itself is the Supernatural Hunt activity. Hunters. Riding out on a hunt.

Witches, demons and mages

How do I become a witch?

A witch brings you in. The route is the Convert to Witchcraft scheme, which a witch runs on someone who is not one. Nobody is born a witch: what passes to children is the Source and Yaga's Blood. Becoming a witch.

How do I become a demon?

Take Minor Demon in the ruler designer. Otherwise there are three routes: be born to a demon with Demonic Heritage, which becomes the trait in your twenties; answer a Magic Eruption with I must harness this power at all costs; or finish the Ascendancy special project. Becoming a demon.

How do I become a mage?

Mage is the capstone of the Arcane tree in the Magic lifestyle. Arcane is closed to a plain witch: it opens with a magic artifact, the Source blood, a place of power, an academy, or a house that has one. Finish the tree and the Mage trait is yours, unless you already hold Warlock or True Witch: you get one of the three. A hunter cannot take it. The Order of the Blue Rose also teaches its acolytes to be Mages. The Magic lifestyle and its trees.

How do I join the Order of the Blue Rose?

You are called; you cannot apply. Each year every temple sends a note and a blue rose to a few people in reach: Mages, people with the Source, members' children, learned witches and scholars. Answer it, pass two tests, take the oath. To be noticed sooner, remember the name when you hear the rumour, or use Seek Out the Order. Who the Order calls.

Setup and older saves

What is the load order with A Game of Thrones?

A Game of Thrones, then AGOT submods, then The Long Night & Azor Ahai if you use it, then Supernatural, then the compatibility patch last. Supernatural runs on the base game alone. Do not load it with Witchcraft or V&L. Load order, in full.

Why does House Hellion have hundreds of members in my old save?

Before 2.35 (10 September 2026), every witch a witch hunt invented was born into House Hellion, which is the demons' house. New witches have no dynasty. Nothing moves the old ones out, so a save from before the fix keeps them. House Hellion.

Supernatural puts vampires, werewolves, hybrids, witches, demons and the hunters who go after them into Crusader Kings III as characters rather than as scenery. A vampire is a lord with a court and a title. A werewolf is somebody's son. The hunter who arrives at your gate was trained by a man whose name is in his trait tooltip.

The design rule the mod keeps coming back to is that power is visible. Killing is not free: a body takes something out of the county it was found in, and so does a feed somebody talks about, and what it takes is the thing a hunter can read months later. Compelling people leaves traces too: three botched compulsions in one county inside five years, and the county starts to notice that nobody remembers that week. Killing somebody leaves a body, and the morning after you choose what the body looks like. That chain, from an act to a mark to a lord's letter to a man at your door, is what 2.35 built, and it is the spine of the whole mod now.

Underneath that there are six lifestyles with ten perk trees between them, a trait for each kind of creature, most of them with a power track and rank names of their own, six legendary wolf hides, a secret society of Mages, ten men-at-arms regiments, twenty-six game rules, and a large body of witchcraft content carried forward from another mod. The pieces are covered in order below; if you only want one part of it, the contents rail on the left will take you there and every heading has a link you can paste into a chat.

Before you playInstall and load order#

On Steam, subscribe and enable it. It runs on the base game and needs no other mod. Which version to use depends on your version of Crusader Kings III.

Without Steam, take the archive from the releases page. If you took the Source code (zip), open the CK3SupernaturalMod-<version>/ folder first and take the two items out of it. You need both supernatural_mod.mod and the supernatural_mod folder, dropped side by side into the CK3 mod folder, so that you end up with mod\supernatural_mod.mod and mod\supernatural_mod\. The full walkthrough with paths for all three operating systems is on the download page.

If the launcher does not show the mod after a manual install, it is almost always one of two things: only the folder was copied, and the launcher reads .mod files rather than folders; or the .mod file is a launcher-generated copy whose path= points at an absolute folder on somebody else's machine. The portable form reads path="mod/supernatural_mod" and has no remote_file_id line. The descriptor.mod inside the mod folder is a different file and is not the one the launcher reads.

Which version for which game#

Versions 2.36 to 2.38 are built for Crusader Kings III 1.20; 2.38 was tested on 1.20.0.4. Version 2.35a and earlier were built for 1.19, and 2.35a crashes on 1.20 as a new game loads in.

With A Game of Thrones: as of 1 October 2026 AGOT had not been updated for 1.20, and the compatibility patch follows AGOT's update. Until then, play AGOT on CK3 1.19 with v2.35a, the last version built for 1.19.

Load order with A Game of Thrones#

  1. A Game of Thrones
  2. AGOT submods
  3. AGOT: The Long Night & Azor Ahai (optional)
  4. Supernatural
  5. AGOT: Supernatural & The Long Night Compatibility Patch, always last

The patch is a separate subscription. It exists because Supernatural and The Long Night define the same twenty-two entries: marriages, secrets, disinheritance, the witch ritual, birth events, schemes, abductions and more. The patch merges them so neither mod silently erases the other's work. One of those entries, the marriage-acceptance rule, was behind multiplayer desyncs in testing.

Do not run these alongside it

Witchcraft and V&L are the important two. Supernatural already carries that content, so loading either next to it puts two copies of the same files against each other and whichever loads later wins.

Also known not to get along: AGOT: Battlefield Duels, Better Barbershop, Asia Expansion Project, Cultural Armies Pack.

Compatibility and what it overrides lists which base-game files the mod replaces, which is what decides whether another mod will conflict with it. For multiplayer, see Multiplayer.

The blood

Vampires: how one is made, how it feeds, and what its maker can ask of it.

Vampires

The bloodVampires#

A vampire is immortal, cannot have children, does not age past the day they turned, and is the only creature in the mod with a relationship to the person who made them that the game itself tracks and prices.

VampireKnown VampireTrue VampireVampire lifestyle

The trait gives +3 intrigue, +2 stewardship, +1 learning and +10 prowess before any rank, immunity to non-epidemic disease, and no prowess loss from age. It renames itself by rank as the Vampirism track fills.

Becoming one#

Every route into vampirism runs through one hidden event, which is why they all behave consistently: it refuses anyone who already holds a supernatural or hunter trait, records the age you were when it happened so the visual ageing rules have something to freeze, clears any curable disease you were carrying, and decides whether you end up with a hidden secret or an openly Known Vampire trait.

Ask a vampire for it. The interaction is Ask to be Turned, used on a vampire you know to be one. Only a player uses it; the AI never asks. On acceptance you get their blood first: the Vampire's Blood modifier, worth +0.4 health, +2 max combat roll, 20 per cent faster stress loss and 15 per cent more lifestyle experience, and a flag that the rest of the system reads. The blood wears off after three years, but the change comes before that, at a random point within a year. If you change your mind first, Ask to Not be Turned stops the change. It does not remove the blood.

Be offered it. Two interactions, both used by a vampire or a hybrid. Offer Blood gives the blood to someone who does not have it, and on its own it never turns anyone. Turn Vampire finishes the change at once, and only appears against someone who already carries the blood. An AI vampire uses both if it rules a county or more; an AI hybrid uses neither. The AI weighs a sick recipient as a better candidate than a healthy one, and a friend, child, spouse or soulmate far above a stranger.

Fall in battle with the blood in you. While you carry vampire blood, a knight or commander who would have taken a fatal blow instead comes off the field alive, at roughly two-thirds of one per cent per combat pulse for a prowess-15 knight, scaled by prowess and by how badly the fight is going. Three days later you wake with no pain and a closed wound, and the chain gives you about a month:

BeatWhenWhat happens
You Should Have Died3 days after the fallOne option. You get up. If somebody felled you, the game remembers who.
What the Blood Wants+14 daysThe choice: drain a man on your own side who will not be missed, ask the vampire who gave you the blood if they are standing there, or refuse.
The Last of It+14 daysOnly if you refused. Feed, or refuse a second time and die of it.

Refusing twice kills you, and the death reads woke from death with a vampire's blood in his veins, would not feed, and died again. Asking the donor is the branch where nobody dies, and it needs them alive, vampiric and standing in the same place you are. If there is nobody suitable to drain, the game creates somebody, so the chain works for a landless adventurer with no court.

Die of natural causes with the blood in you. Vampire blood is wired into the game's own second-chance-at-death rule, alongside the base game's Know Thyself perk. You come back as a vampire instead of dying.

Nobody is born a vampire. A vampire cannot have children, and no setting of the Supernatural: Inheritance/Pregnancy rule produces a vampire child. Birth is a werewolf route.

Start as one. The Vampire trait is in the ruler designer, for 150 points.

The blood on its own. Anyone carrying the blood, AI included, can get the event The Blood. Its second part can turn you, and its riskier answer kills six times in ten.

The random embrace events. Six chains, all of them player-only, fired at three per cent per player character per year when the Supernatural: Events rule allows it. Two are vampire routes, A Night Amongst the Nobles and A Unique Visit. A third, Market Day, can end with you a vampire or a werewolf. They are also the reason a player meets these things at all in a world where the AI mostly does not.

The Embrace Vampirism decision lets you pick it yourself. It needs the Supernatural: Embrace Decisions rule set to On, and that rule is off by default. So does the cure, Abandon Vampirism. The eight decisions.

How the track fills. Every living vampire gets +1 a year and nothing more, which is a hundred years to Elder on the drip alone. The real lever is feeding: a clean feed on the decision pays 15 to 35, a feed that ends in a kill pays the same, the traveller on the road pays 5 to 12 and blood you paid for pays 10 to 20. Surviving a hunter pays the same for every kind: 15 to 30 for beating one on a hunt, 8 to 15 for looking him in the eye, 5 to 12 for going to ground or sending him to another county, and 4 to 10 for running when you are warned. The century events pay perk points rather than track experience.

Feeding: Satiate Thirst and the Feeding activity#

There are two feeding systems. The Satiate Thirst decision is the quick one: a seven-year cooldown for a player, a three-day wait, and a short event chain with three ways to go about it. The Feeding activity is the long one: you travel to a town, you can bring up to five other vampires, and it runs over about six weeks with a visible success bar.

The decision offers a courtier from your own realm, a traveller on the road, or blood you buy for a little gold. Taking the courtier is the highest-paying and the riskiest: seven times in ten it goes cleanly, twice in ten the courtier dies, and once in ten you are seen and the secret starts moving. Higher Vampirism shifts weight out of both failure branches and into the clean one, so power makes you tidier as well as stronger.

The activity is the set piece. It needs a province with a city or tribal holding within reach of your court, and you choose two things before you ride:

Who the victim is. The game looks in the local holder's court first, then a neighbouring county's court, then your own court as a last resort, and creates somebody on the spot if none of those work. Your own people are the last choice, not the first.

Afterwards. Everyone who attended carries Recently Fed for three years, worth +1 to four skills and +10 attraction, and cannot be invited to another feeding while it lasts. A victim who survives carries Bloodloss for three years, which a hunter can find and question.

Sire and progeny#

Whoever's blood made you is your Sire, and you are their Progeny. The relation is asymmetric on purpose: the progeny likes the maker by +400, the maker likes the progeny by +150. It is set on every route in, which was not true before 2.33; a vampire made by the older paths had a sire recorded in their tooltip and no actual relation, so none of the things below worked for them.

What the bond is for is Invoke the Bond, an interaction a sire can use on their progeny once every eight years. It has twenty orders and each one is paid for out of the sire's own Vampirism track, which is the whole design: leaning on your bloodline costs you rank.

The bond does quieter work as well. A sire and a progeny can always invite each other to a feeding, without either of them having to hold the other's secret, because a maker knows what their progeny is. An AI creature will not sell out its own blood to a hunter. And a hybrid's death on a hunt can be remembered by two different makers, the one who gave the blood and the one who did the clawing, where a vampire's can only be remembered by one.

Compulsion (Compel Mortal)#

Compulsion is the vampire's reach into other people's heads. The interaction is Compel Mortal; Compulsion is the name of the perk that strengthens it. It is available to vampires and hybrids, has a six-month global cooldown and a three-month cooldown against the same person, and it works on mortals and werewolves only. Vampires, hybrids, demons and witches cannot be compelled at all. Werewolves can, and they resist hard and cost an extra 5 track experience on top of whatever you asked for.

You also have to be able to reach them. The target must be in your realm, your liege's realm or a bordering one, or share a court with you, or be standing in the same place you are, or be at the same activity. A feast is a compulsion range extender.

Resistance is built from your own power, which climbs steeply with the track and climbs about sixty per cent faster again with the Compulsion perk, set against the target's will: learning resists, brave and stubborn resist hard, craven and trusting fold, and a hunter resists by −20 before you start and a further −10 for every ten points of Monster-Hunter track he has. A hunter at the top of his track is effectively immune.

Two things happen afterwards. A refused compulsion hands your victim your secret if you have one. And a compulsion used in a county that carries your own trail can bury an incident, or leave a hole in the county's memory if it goes badly. Covering your own kills has the odds.

How a vampire looks#

Turning lightens a vampire's skin by one step. That is the Supernatural: Paleness rule, which ships as "Default (Vampires)" and can be extended to hybrids or switched off. How the portrait ages over the centuries is the Supernatural: Ageing rule (Game rules).

There are no fangs. The mod has no fangs portrait, although its event text mentions them. The game files still carry text for a "Supernatural: Visual Fangs" rule, but the rule itself was removed, so it never appears on the game rules screen.

Rank does not show. Nothing in the portrait code reads the Vampirism track, so a Fledgling and an Elder are drawn the same; the rank shows in the trait name and the modifiers.

The Originals#

Eight characters are placed in history as the Mikaelson family: Mikael and Esther, and their children Freja, Finn, Elijah, Niklaus, Rebecca and Kol. Esther and Freja are witches. Six of them turn on the same day in 866, and five days later Mikael founds a landless adventurer camp and takes the others into it, so the Originals start a campaign as a travelling company rather than a realm. Niklaus is the mod's one placed hybrid, and every hybrid that has ever existed in any save descends from somebody he or a player made.

Mechanically, being one of them does three things: an Original at a feeding is worth +15 on the success chance, the male Originals wear no beard and no headgear, and any vampire attending a feeding with one has a four-in-five chance of a short event chain about meeting them, once per character ever.

The moonWerewolves#

A werewolf is mortal. It ages and scars like anyone else. What it has against an ordinary death is fifty years of added life expectancy at a full track, twenty-five more if a hide chooses it, and thirty at full Hide Bond.

The trait gives +2 martial, +2 diplomacy, +1 learning and +15 prowess before any rank, plus immunity to non-epidemic disease and +10 life expectancy. It renames itself by rank as the Lycanthropy track fills, and it is the only creature whose power reads as fighting strength first.

Becoming one: the bite, the wound and birth#

Being clawed does not make you a werewolf. It gives you the Infected Wound, a health trait with its own Infection track that sits on you for one to three years and then resolves. The exact date is rolled the moment the wound lands, somewhere between 365 and 1,095 days out, and nothing you do moves it.

While you carry it you are visibly sick. The base wound costs 0.75 health, 2 martial, 1 intrigue, 3 prowess and a slice of fertility, and it disables combat leadership outright for the whole incubation, not just the bad end of it. Each tier of the Infection track adds more on top, and the tiers rename the trait as you climb: Infected Wound, Festering Wound, Virulent Wound, Ravaging Wound, Consuming Wound. At the top rung you are carrying −2.5 health, −10 martial, −15 prowess and a fifth more stress gain.

The track moves three ways. It creeps up on its own, three points a month, which over a long incubation is most of a full track. Roughly every three to four months an event fires from a pool of nine, and each one is a choice between pushing the infection down and paying for it in stress, or letting it climb and taking the comfort. And one event in that pool, The Wolf Stirs, asks you the real question: do you want this. Answering yes lowers the infection. Answering no raises it. The flag you set matters far more than the number, and this is the single most counter-intuitive thing in the chain.

Some of the other events in the pool matter more than they look. A Healer's Hands is the biggest single drop available, at up to 20 points, at a 15 per cent risk that the physician works out what you are. A Hunter Grows Suspicious is where naming the werewolf who clawed you hands a hunter a named target, and is one of the ways hunts start. The Wound Worsens can take a limb. The One Who Clawed You opens a short relationship chain with the wolf who made you that can end in friendship, a lover or a soulmate.

How it ends. On the appointed day The Fever Breaks fires, you pick one of up to seven stances, and the game rolls one of six outcomes against your infection level, your traits and the flags you set along the way:

OutcomeWhat happens
Something MoreYou turn, and start at 30 Lycanthropy. Grants Strong if you lack it.

A Hard TurningYou turn at zero, plus 30 stress.

Low infection is what buys the clean turnings; high infection feeds both the scarred reprieve and the death. Yielding forecloses the good non-turn entirely. Resisting makes both non-turns much likelier and also feeds the death bucket, because resisting is what drove your infection up in the first place.

Hides and packs in the fever. Three things from 2.37 reach into this chain. When a hide is near, the pool gains a ninth event, It Lies Beside the Bed, and The Fever Breaks offers Reach for the hide, which always ends in the best turning (how to get a hide). A wolf carried through a Werewolf Gathering gets likelier clean turnings and the stance Let the pack carry me. And a hide that carried you through the fever counts 20 toward its fit with you.

The Confer Death game rule does not protect you here. It is read only by the two hybrid turnings, and by nothing in the werewolf wound chain. A player who sets it to Off will still lose characters to The Change Consumes You.

The three ways to hand it out. None of them costs gold or piety.

  • Offer Gift. You ask; they decide. The wound starts at 5 to 15 infection and is flagged voluntary, which helps at the climax. The AI starts at −25 against and is moved most by fear of its own death, by being your child, spouse or soulmate, and by dread. A hook auto-accepts it, and the hook is consumed.
  • Force Gift. Hostile, no reply, straight to a wound at 40 infection, tier three before the first event fires, flagged forced, which pushes the roll toward the rough turning and toward death. You need to be physically near them: same location and same army, activity or landless status, or they are your vassal, prisoner, courtier or guest. It costs you nothing but gives you +5 dread and puts the victim on a potential-rival footing. A player can do it once every five years; an AI once every ten.
  • Ask for the Gift. The mirror, and player-only: the AI never asks. You need to know the target is a werewolf. The wound starts low and voluntary, same as being offered it.

There is no equivalent of the vampire's Ask to Not be Turned. Once a wound is in you, it ends in a turning, one of the two reprieves, or death.

Birth. Children of werewolves are born werewolves, never hybrids. Which parent counts depends on the Supernatural: Inheritance/Pregnancy rule, which ships on the Default (Werewolf Trait Only) setting. A newborn does not get the trait directly; it gets a flag, and a hidden event decides how much of a head start the bloodline gives it, weighing every parent and grandparent. Two supernatural grandparents is a meaningfully stronger child than none.

How the track fills. Every living werewolf gets +1 a year, which is a hundred years to Elder on the drip alone. The main source is Satiate Hunger, on a five-year cooldown for a player. Twenty to twenty-five years of consistent hunting is a realistic run to the cap.

Satiate Hunger#

Satiate Hunger is the decision, and it is free: no gold, no piety, no prestige. Three days later you pick a route. Hunting your own people is the high-return one and the only one with teeth in it: 70 per cent a clean hunt, 20 per cent you kill a vassal, 9 per cent you are seen and become a Known Werewolf, and 2 per cent a hunter puts a silver arrow in you. Rank shifts that in your favour: at Lycanthropy 15, 35 and 55, weight moves out of the two bad results and into the clean hunt, 30 points in all by 55. Running the border and crossing another wolf's ground are the safe options: lower experience, no death roll, a suspicious neighbour, and in the border case a prowl mark on the county.

Shifting#

Werewolves and hybrids get a self-targeted Shapeshift interaction with three forms. It is free, instant, has no cooldown and no duration, so you stay in the form you chose until you choose another. The AI can never open it; the block that would tell an AI when to shift is present in the file but unreachable.

FormWhat it gives
HumanNothing. This is the absence of the other two.
Half-Wolf+3 prowess, +2 martial, +2 learning, −2 diplomacy, −1 stewardship
Werewolf+6 prowess, +4 martial, −4 diplomacy, −2 stewardship, and you lose your hair

The full wolf form is worth a further +15 to your side of a hunt duel, which is the one place the choice has real weight. Set against that, a shapeshifted body is unmistakable: if you are killed while shifted, the corpse reads as a werewolf no matter what you actually were.

What shifting does not do is change your model. There is no wolf portrait, no wolf head, no new skin. The only visible change is going bald in full wolf form, plus the modifier on your character sheet. The prowess in both forms is the kind that deliberately does not alter your portrait's build.

One other thing can put you in wolf form: during a hunt, when a hunter is at your door, you can answer it as the wolf. That is the only timed shift in the mod, thirty days, and it costs you your secret with every hunter attending.

Maker and get#

The mod tracks who clawed whom with a pair of relations, Clawed and Clawed By. They are identifiers and nothing more: both are worth zero opinion. Compare the vampire's Sire and Progeny, worth +400 and +150. The werewolf's sentiment comes from a separate pair of permanent opinion modifiers instead: +50 grateful if the turning was voluntary, −50 resentful if it was forced. A forced victim can be linked to the wolf who made him and be his rival at the same time.

Maker and get also count as kin when a hunter comes: see Who stands with the creature. Standing groups of wolves are Packs.

Packs and the Werewolf Gathering#

Everything here applies to hybrids too; the differences are under Hybrids. A pack is a standing group with an alpha, a Second and up to 25 wolves, the alpha included, and the wolves have a place among each other that rises and falls.

All of it sits behind the Supernatural: Packs game rule, which is on by default. With it off there are no packs, but the Werewolf Gathering still runs and wolves can still Challenge each other.

Pack Standing#

Every werewolf and hybrid has a second track, Pack Standing, from 0 to 100. It decides who may found a pack, who stands Second, and who may Challenge the alpha. A wolf born to it starts at 25; a wolf made by a bite starts at nothing. A werewolf who becomes a hybrid keeps his standing. On a save from before 2.37, a wolf born to it gets his 25 the first time he founds a pack or finishes a Werewolf Gathering.

Packs recruit. Each year a packless adult wolf has a 20% chance of being asked by an AI alpha with room who knows what he is, does not dislike him and is not his rival. The alpha must be his kin, or tied to him by the claw, or be within reach and either share his faith or culture or think well of him; the wolf must not think badly of the alpha either. Kin, the claw and a Champion alpha weigh more, and the same alpha asks the same wolf at most once in two years. The question comes as An Invitation.

An AI wolf asks to run with another pack, or leaves his own, only for a reason: he has no pack, he and his alpha have no use for each other, or a friend or lover runs in the other. Asked with Ask to Run With Their Pack, an AI alpha whose pack has only one or two wolves is readier to say yes.

When an alpha dies, the pack goes to his co-alpha if he has one, then to the highest-standing Champion in it, then to the wolf who killed him if that wolf is in the pack, and then to the highest-standing wolf left.

What a pack gives#

  • Runs With a Pack: +1% werewolf and hybrid lifestyle experience and +0.5% to every lifestyle for each wolf in it, so a full pack of 25 is worth +25% and +12.5%. A pack with a True Alpha in it counts double. The Pack Mentality perk adds half as much again.
  • Every year: +5 Pack Standing and +2 Lycanthropy, or +3 under an alpha who is a Champion, with one more for two Champions in the pack and two more for three or more. A Champion in a pack gains +5 Hide Bond a year rather than +2.
  • Two Champions in one pack give every member Two Spirits Run Together: +2 prowess and +10% werewolf and hybrid lifestyle experience. Three or more give The Old Ones Gather: +4 prowess, +0.2 health and +20%.
  • On a hunt, a pack-mate in the county comes through the door as kin, worth 12 off the hunter's side (see Who stands with the creature).

The pack shows in the Situations window. The alpha owns its panel, and since 2.38 every other player in the pack sees a copy listing the alpha, the Second, a co-alpha, the Champions and the wolves. A button on the left of the screen, Your Pack, opens the window.

Once a year each pack has a 20% chance of a moment with a pack nearby: a scent on the border that ends in a rivalry or a truce, a wolf from the other pack asking to run with yours, two young wolves fighting, two wolves from different packs finding each other, or word of where a hide is. A player alpha is asked when the moment is his to decide, and a moment never moves or wounds a player's character, or makes him a lover, without asking.

Challenge#

One wolf can Challenge another he knows to be a wolf if they are rivals, if he has the On The Prowl perk (werewolf tree) or the Challenge perk (hybrid tree), or if they share a pack and his standing is at least 25. To challenge your own alpha you must be the Second, or the Second must have lost to the alpha in the last two years, and you need standing 50. Neither of you can be badly wounded. You can challenge once a year, and the same wolf once every two. A Challenge can be sent to a wolf who will refuse it; refusing costs him 10 standing. An AI wolf refuses a Champion when he has no hide himself, and a fight he would likely lose, unless he is bold or it is his rival.

The fight is decided the way every fight between creatures is, both sides in wolf form. Nobody dies of a Challenge. The loser is wounded, loses 10 standing and thinks −10 of the winner for five years. The winner gains 15 standing, as long as the loser stood no more than 10 below him. Beat your alpha and the pack is yours. If the alpha beats his Second, any wolf in the pack at standing 50 may challenge him for the next two years, or until the pack has a new alpha. A co-alpha stays co-alpha only beside the spouse the Unification joined them to.

Why an AI wolf challenges. Never for nothing. He challenges his own alpha only when the alpha is too weak to lead or is cruel to the better wolves of the pack. Too weak: he would likely beat him; the alpha is infirm, incapable or blind; the pack holds the alpha 15 standing below him; the alpha is craven; or the hunters know the alpha's name while the challenger still hides. Cruel: the alpha is sadistic, callous, arbitrary, deceitful or a kinslayer, the challenger is none of these, and he has the virtue that stands against it (compassionate, just or honest), or he has come to dislike the alpha, or two of the pack have. Another pack's alpha he takes on only over a feud: a rival, a grudge, or real hatred. Any other wolf he challenges over a feud, or to climb his own pack against a wolf who stands as high and whom he would beat. He picks fights he can win, takes worse odds against a cruel alpha, and never challenges his spouse, lover or friend. When an AI wolf challenges you, he tells you why.

The Werewolf Gathering#

An activity for werewolves and hybrids. Any wolf who is a ruler or a landless adventurer can host one, every five years, for the same gold as a camp party. It is held at a county seat in reach, a forest or taiga by preference, with up to 12 guests: werewolves you are close to, your own pack, other alphas you know of, and anyone you know to be in the bite's fever. Hybrids are an optional invitation.

The True Alpha, the Unification and the Blood Pact#

The True Alpha rises at most once in a hundred years, anywhere in the world. He has to be at Pack Standing 100, not the alpha of his pack, never have killed a wolf, and carry at least two of Just, Honest, Compassionate, Brave and Diligent. Each pack rolls 5% a year for its best candidate. When he rises he either takes his pack, and the old alpha thinks well of him for it, or leaves to found his own and takes some of it with him. A True Alpha's pack counts double for its lifestyle bonus, he has Hide Strength even without a hide, and Alpha wants him more than anyone.

The Unification joins two packs whose alphas are married to each other. It is held at a Werewolf Gathering under the intent Hold the Unification, needs a third wolf there to stand as elder and the two packs to come to no more than 25 together, and only works if there are no secrets between husband and wife. The packs become one, the spouse becomes co-alpha, and every wolf in it is Evolved: he fights as if in wolf form even when he is not, worth +15 in a fight between creatures and on a hunt. If either alpha is a hybrid, every wolf also gains +10 life expectancy. An AI host never joins a player's pack to his own, and a player whose pack is joined is told.

The Blood Pact is sworn at a Gathering when another pack's alpha comes to your fire. Between two rulers it is a real alliance; either way it is +20 opinion both ways for twenty years.

The Six Hides#

Six old wolf skins, each with a spirit and a will of its own: Silverback, Timber, Tundra, Graybeard, Midnight and Alpha. A hide chooses a werewolf or hybrid who fits it, and that wolf becomes its Champion. Everything here applies to hybrids too; the differences are under Hybrids.

A hide is an artifact in the trinket slot. Only an adult werewolf or hybrid can put one on, and only the hide's own Champion gets its power: anyone else who wears it gets 0.3 prestige a month and nothing more. One body carries one hide. A Champion who comes into a second one has to choose between them in Two Wolves: the hide he keeps gives him +10 Hide Bond, and the one he puts aside will not have him for five years.

The six, and who they choose#

A hide reads your traits. It takes you if you have at least one trait it likes and more that it likes than it dislikes. Failing that, it adds up a fit score, +15 for every trait it likes and −20 for every one it dislikes, plus half your Lycanthropy, and takes you anyway at 40 or more. Being carried through the bite's fever by a hide adds 20 to that score, and so does standing in a Werewolf Gathering's Circle (40 for a player). Alpha is the exception: it takes only a leader, meaning the alpha of a pack or a ruler of duke rank or higher. With the Packs rule off, a True Werewolf or a wolf at full Lycanthropy also counts.

What a Champion gets#

The worn power is the same for all six: +20 prowess and a further +25% prowess, +25 life expectancy, +0.5 health, +5% knight effectiveness, and +1 to the size cap of the mod's monster regiments. The differences between the hides are in the Champion traits and in what each one's Hide Bond track adds.

  • He hears magic. A Champion counts as having Witchcraft's Magical Senses, so a spell cast near him rings. Once a year he can follow the ringing and go after the caster: a fight between the two that never kills, where winning wounds the caster and gives him +5 Hide Bond, and losing wounds him instead.
  • A Champion who practises magic, as a Mage, a True Witch or one born with the Source, regains a great deal of power every year in the Bond, and a Mage among them gains a point of Combat Magic as well. Other wolves get nothing extra.
  • He can raise the Werewolves regiment without the Lycanthropy 50 it normally needs, and can found a pack at any Pack Standing.
  • A known Champion is feared: +15 dread while he carries the Known Werewolf or Known Hybrid trait, +25 for Timber's.
  • Old age does not break him. Once a year the hide clears Infirm, Clouded Eyes, Faltering Heart, Withering Mind, Fragile Bones, Incapable and Blind, the same cleanup that keeps vampires young, and it clears them the day he is chosen.
  • An immortal Champion, which in practice means a hybrid, also gets Ageless Wolf: +20% werewolf and hybrid lifestyle experience.
  • The hide speaks his tongue. At Hide Bond 25 it learns his culture's language, and every later Champion of that hide speaks every language it has learned.
  • Nobody can take it from him while he lives. A worn hide carries no claims, and any transfer away from a living Champion is undone.

Hide Strength#

In a fight between creatures a Champion counts for more. His side is multiplied by his Hide Strength: ×1.5 at Hide Bond 0, rising to ×2.5 at 100. Silverback and Alpha add a further 0.25, and so does being the True Alpha, so the ceiling is ×3. A True Alpha who wears no hide is ×1.25.

It applies to a hunt's final fight when the hunter has come for a Champion, to a Challenge, to the fight over a hide at a Gathering, to following the ringing, and to two Champions meeting on a battlefield. It does not apply to cutting down ordinary knights in battle, which works on its own budget.

The numbers: how a fight between creatures is decided

Each side's weight is 50, plus the prowess difference between them (at most 25 either way), plus 0.6 × their creature track, plus 15 in wolf form, plus 10 for the alpha of a pack. That is multiplied by Hide Strength, and by 1.6 for a hybrid. The chance to win is A² ÷ (A² + B²), never below 2% or above 98%. The game never shows the number. The tooltip says The fight leans toward and a name when one side has 65% or better, and The two are evenly matched. between 35% and 65%.

Four ways to take a hide out of a shrine:

  • Hold the county, or be its holder's top liege. The game lets either take a relic down from a holy site, and a hide is no different. AI werewolf lords have a decision for it.
  • Siege it. When a siege of the county completes and you are a werewolf or hybrid, or a wolf commands your army there, A Hide in the Shrine lets the wolf take it. The game's own siege looting can get to it first.

  • Send for a Hide. A decision for a wolf who knows where a hide is kept. Then you decide whether your man tries to take it, with the same intrigue duel and the same price for failing.

A lost hide goes looking. It wakes after 5 to 20 years (Alpha, 15 to 40) and picks a wolf who fits it. If that wolf is a player who rules a county, a great white wolf is seen in his capital (Something in Your Woods); hunt it down with a great success and the hide is his. Anyone else simply finds it, in It Came Out of the Trees.

A hide does not lie idle. A hide that spends ten years with an AI who is not its Champion (a mortal, a wolf it refused, or a Champion keeping it as a spare) slips away one winter and is lost, to wake again for a wolf it fits. A hunter's trophy, a shrine's relic and a hide a player holds are never taken.

There are three more ways into one. At a Werewolf Gathering, a hide nobody wears that someone has brought can choose a wolf at the fire (The Choosing). In the bite's fever, Reach for the hide is offered when a hide is close, and always ends in the best turning. And a hunter who kills a Champion is left holding the hide in What the Beast Left, where he can keep it, give it to his liege, give it to his church, or try to burn it.

Losing a hide#

  • Death. The hide goes to whoever killed him. Failing that it goes to his heir, and failing that it is lost. His house is left with a claim on it, and so is his pack's alpha.
  • Ask to Unbind the Hide. A Mage or True Witch who knows what you are can cut the bond, if they agree to, for 150 gold. It leaves a wound, never a fatal one. You keep the hide, but it will not have you for ten years.
  • Abandon Lycanthropy or Abandon Being a Hybrid ends it, and takes you out of your pack too. Both sit behind the Embrace Decisions rule, which ships off.
  • Two Wolves, when he takes the other hide.
  • Tear the Hide Away. A Mage's spell to rip the Vade Maecum's page out of a worn hide. If it works on a Champion who was already maimed or badly wounded, he dies of it, and by the rule above the hide goes to the Mage.

When a hide passes from a wolf in one pack to a wolf in another, the two alphas fall into a Hide Feud: −30 opinion both ways for ten years, and worse relations between their houses.

The cure#

Abandon Lycanthropy is a decision with no cost and no cooldown, and it takes everything: the trait, Known Werewolf, True Werewolf, all eight lifestyle perks and your secret. Since 2.37 it also ends a Champion's bond with his hide, which stays in his hands, and takes him out of his pack. It is behind the Embrace Decisions game rule, which ships off. With that rule at its default there is no cure in the game at all.

The cure leaves two things behind. If you were in wolf form when you were cured you stay in wolf form, and bald, permanently. The only thing that un-shifts a werewolf is the Shapeshift interaction, and that interaction will not show for someone who is no longer a werewolf. And nothing at all removes an Infected Wound before it resolves: no decision, no interaction, no physician. Once wounded you carry the health penalty and the wait, and the wait includes a death bucket.

A hybrid is the mod's most powerful creature and its rarest. One is placed in history. None can be born. Every other hybrid has to be made, one character at a time, and at the default settings the turning can kill.

Making one#

Two interactions make a hybrid, and both start from a werewolf. A vampire can never become one. Make Hybrid is used by a vampire or a hybrid on a werewolf who already has vampire blood in them, so the sequence is: be a werewolf, be given the blood with Offer Blood, then be turned. The hybrid Ask to be Turned runs the other way: a werewolf asks a vampire or a hybrid, and needs no blood first.

A human with no creature trait has two other routes: the ruler designer, and the Become a Hybrid decision, which needs the Supernatural: Embrace Decisions rule set to On.

The two turnings are the only place the Supernatural: Confer Death rule is read, and at its default they are dangerous. Make Hybrid starts at 40 live, 60 die, and each point of health above a pivot moves 10 from die to live. The pivot is 4 at the default, 5.5 on More Death and 2.5 on Less Death, so living is the likelier result above health 5, 6.5 and 3.5. Ask to be Turned starts at 50 live, 50 die, with the same pivots. With the rule set to Off, the turning always succeeds.

What a hybrid can do#

A hybrid can do nearly everything both parents can. It shapeshifts. It compels, paying from its own Vampirism track at the same thresholds a vampire pays. It feeds, on its own decision with a seven-year cooldown rather than the werewolf's five, which splits fifty-fifty between the vampire chain and the wolf chain. It claws, though no AI hybrid ever will, because the AI side of both claw interactions requires the plain werewolf trait. It regenerates: the cleanups that strip disfigurement and the traits of old age run on vampires and hybrids. Among werewolves only a hide's Champion gets them.

Like vampires, demons and witches, a hybrid cannot be compelled. Werewolves can be, at a cost (Compulsion).

In a hunt a hybrid carries +10 on its own account, and the game reads whichever of its two tracks is higher when it prices the fight, the lord's investigation and the prestige for the kill. If it dies shifted, the body reads as a werewolf; otherwise it reads as a vampire. The mod never gives a hybrid a death of its own: in the old confrontation chain it dies as one or the other on a coin flip.

Hides and packs#

Packs and the Six Hides work for a hybrid as they do for a werewolf. These are the differences:

  • A hybrid Champion does not age, so he also gets Ageless Wolf: +20% werewolf and hybrid lifestyle experience (What a Champion gets).
  • A hybrid counts ×1.6 in a fight between creatures (Hide Strength).
  • The Challenge and Pack Leader perks are in the hybrid's Lycanthropy tree. Challenge lets him Challenge any wolf he knows; Pack Leader lets him found a pack at Pack Standing 40.
  • A werewolf who becomes a hybrid keeps his Pack Standing.
  • A hybrid alpha in a Unification gives every wolf of the joined pack +10 life expectancy (The Unification).
  • Hybrids are an optional invitation to a Werewolf Gathering.

The hybrid lifestyle#

The hybrid lifestyle is tied with the vampire's for the most expensive in the mod, and it buys eighteen perks across two trees, Vampirism and Lycanthropy, where the vampire has ten in one and the werewolf eight. Both capstones together grant the two traits the vampire and werewolf capstones grant separately, and the master focus needs both of them. Finishing it takes roughly 27,000 lifestyle experience and two full tracks. Lycanthropy is the slower of the two tracks to fill.

The cure#

Abandon Being a Hybrid works like the werewolf cure and sits behind the same game rule. It takes the trait, the known trait and the secret, ends a Champion's bond with his hide and takes him out of his pack. It leaves all eighteen perks and both capstone traits in place.

The False Mage#

Some characters carry the Mage trait without the power: a secret, False Mage, says they are pretending. A False Mage shows the trait but fails the true-mage check that most mage content uses: no Magical Research, no Teach Sorcery, no research spells, no mage contracts. A few things look only at the trait, among them Mage Duel and a Gnostic Council appointment. A player can choose to become one when an event offers it; one True Witch in ten is offered it six months to three years after the capstone; and two in ten court mages the game generates are frauds. Exposure strips the Mage trait, costs a prestige level and five years of Liar Liar, and a liege who employed them as court mage holds it against them for ten years. Taking the Magical Experiments perk ends the pretence the other way: the secret lapses, and the perk takes the Mage trait away with it.

Spells#

There are 54 magic interactions, most of them spells, and almost none costs gold, piety or prestige: Recall from the Gnostic Council can cost 5,000 prestige, and a refused Mana Siphon or cyrograph costs prestige. The price of a spell is its cooldown, its stress, its power, and the risk of what the target does with it.

Since 2.38, 27 of them, the friendly and diplomatic ones, sit in the Supernatural menu beside the mod's own interactions. Curses and attacks stay under Hostile, and the witch trial under Prison.

Three things can happen to a spell you cast. It can be countered, by a mage who took Counter-spells, or by a court mage protecting his liege's household. It can be stopped outright by an antimagic seal. Or, if the target is a Mage, a demon or Yaga, it can be deflected into a mage duel, which is a different event entirely and not one you chose to start. You also cannot cast while you are already duelling.

The spells themselves run from a curse and a domination at the low end through healing, power strikes, aura enhancement, mind scanning and shadow steps, up to plague, firestorm, tornado, earthquake and teleportation at the far end of the mage tracks. Cooldowns vary widely and are the main limit: Power Strike is a day, Domination is a week, Hypercognition is eight years, and an Earthquake cannot be aimed at the same person twice inside twenty.

A mage learns spells four ways. The Learn new spell decision needs a true mage holding a county with the Academy of Magic, runs every fifteen years, gives +2 Secret Knowledge, and draws one spell you do not know: Bless the Land, Healing, Shadow Step, Plague, Hypercognition, Aura Reading and Thorn Walls are equally likely, Teleportation a little less, Fire Storm rarely. A mage can Teach Spells to another mage every ten years. Magical Research can end in a new spell. And the Arcane tree's Caster perk gives Power Strike and Aura Enhancement outright, and a character who is not yet a Mage also gets the choice of a third spell or another benefit.

Potions#

There is no brewing screen. Potions are artifacts in your inventory with a hidden marker, and there are eleven of them.

You brew with the Create Potion decision, which needs either the witch trait plus Herbalist, or the Advanced Alchemy perk, and, as of 2.34, the hunter tree's Combat Spells perk, which is Supernatural's own addition reaching into the Witchcraft layer. It costs gold by your highest title: 10 for a baron or someone landless, 20 for a count, 40 for a duke, 100 for a king and 150 for an emperor, all halved at Alchemy 30. The cooldown is four years, three at Alchemy 70.

Create Potion is a menu, and most of it is not potions. It offers a Poison, a Love Potion or a Health Elixir, each a three-year modifier on you rather than something in your inventory. Only a Mage or a True Witch gets the fourth choice, I will experiment, which makes one of the potion artifacts seven times in ten; a brewer who is both a herbalist and has Advanced Alchemy never fails it. So a witch-herbalist or a hunter can brew, but never one of the eleven potions. The experiment rolls Regeneration, Magic, Mutagen, Unknown, Youth, Charisma, Might, Learning or Fertility; Mind-Reading only for a Mage or Yaga; and Liquid Aether, a crafting reagent rather than a drink, only for a true mage. At Alchemy 100 a Mage chooses the potion outright.

The player drinks through a self-targeted interaction with a one-day cooldown. The ten separate drink decisions in the files are all AI-only and you will never see them.

Two worth knowing before you drink them. Regeneration clears thirty-eight different afflictions in one go, from leprosy and the pox through blindness, maiming and disfigurement, and it also takes away New Eyes, if you have it. Mutagen is a real gamble: one time in ten you come out Herculean and clean, most of the time you trade fertility for muscle, and one time in twenty it leaves you weak, infertile and disfigured.

Magical Research#

Magical Research is how a mage gets stronger on purpose. You pick a project, work at it for five months, and if you collect enough breakthroughs you are rewarded with what the project promised: a new spell, a permanent power, a regiment, an artifact, or years of life. It is open to a true mage who holds at least a county and is an adult. A Mage on the Gnostic Council's Keeper of the Reliquary seat can run it every five years instead of ten, and a reminder appears in your alerts when it is ready.

There are two kinds. Magical Research happens where you are standing. A Research Expedition travels to one of the world's three places of power, and plays out as a story at that place instead of a project; see Research Expeditions. How a research runs#

  1. Extra Support. On arrival you choose an assistant, or work alone for a little prestige. The candidates are witches you trust: a friend or soulmate, your heir, a witch you hold a hook on, a mage courtier, another player. The assistant does not travel or attend; they simply help. They learn that you are a witch, take Assisted in Magical Research for twenty years (+1 learning, +10% magic experience), think better of you, and gain Secret Knowledge if they are a mage below 100, or a permanent +1 learning if not. How much they add to your work depends on how good they are: +5 from a mage past 75 Secret Knowledge, +3 past 25, +2 from any other mage, +1 from a True Witch, a demon or an Erudite, nothing from anyone else.
  2. Research Focus. Two days later you pick the project. You may change your mind once, which costs eleven days.
  3. Five months of work. Each month one event from the research pool finds you, plus one more scheduled at the start. On top of that, any night there is a chance that someone spends it on the books: a little stress, and +1 progress.
  4. The verdict. At five months the game counts your progress against what the project needed.
  5. Calibration. Four projects play out a follow-up story here: Time Travel, Design Combat Spell, Artificial Human and Magic Ring. If you finished far ahead, Excess Breakthroughs comes next.

Success, failure and Excess Breakthroughs#

Success gives the project's reward and Secret Knowledge by difficulty: +2 for a one-breakthrough project, +3 for two, +5 for three, +10 for four. Failure costs nothing but the time: you get Learning from Mistakes for sixteen years (+1 learning, and +5 starting progress on your next research) and +1 Secret Knowledge. There is no wound, no death and no lost gold for failing.

Beat the target by more than one whole breakthrough, and Excess Breakthroughs asks what to do with the surplus:

  • Use it: more track experience, more again if you are a Legendary Mage.
  • Record it in the chronicles: unlocks Write a Magical Elaborate, a decision that costs gold and runs every five years.
  • Spread the word: a great deal of prestige, and a one-in-two chance that witch hunters take an interest in you for ten years.

What can go wrong

Research is safe unless you choose risk. The one way it can kill you is Risky Experiment's plain option, 2 in 100. Your witch secret gets out if you take Total Commitment's quickest option, or answer the Arcane Research nickname event as the Enchanter. A tortured prisoner and your assistant both learn it, and spreading word of your breakthroughs draws hunters.

Research Expeditions#

An expedition travels to a place of power: the Academy of Magic, the Temple of the Horned God or the Hut On The Chicken's Leg. You can only plan one to a place you know about: one you hold, one inside your own independent realm, one an event has told you of (Magic Triangulation, Rumors About Magic, a search's Places of power event, the Grand Magus's answer), or all three if your dynasty has the Knowledge Sharing legacy. Each is meant to be visited once in a lifetime. There is no project and no verdict: the place itself is the story, and it pays out as you go.

  • The Academy of Magic is the safe one. A guide shows you round; the grand library and the deeper halls pay skills and track experience, sometimes Liquid Aether, and one of the rooms you may find is worth +5 learning outright. You leave with Visited the Academy of Magic for good: +2 learning, +5 starting progress on every research after.
  • The Temple of the Horned God is three trials, and the last can kill you. Fail the first and you limp home wounded, with +1 learning to show for it. Pass all three and the rewards are the biggest an expedition gives: track experience across the board, Source potential, and claws or physical and mental gifts. The Horned God's own favour goes only to a Yaga who asks for more, or to a Shrewd answer at the chalice.
  • The Hut On The Chicken's Leg is Baba Yaga's. If she lives and the hut is not yours, you may be taken in by Sefia and learn from her (once per dynasty), and Baba Yaga will think much less of you when she hears. If no Yaga lives and you hold the hut's county, you can win a learning duel to break in and become the new Yaga. Either way you leave with Visited the Hut On The Chicken's Leg (+1 intrigue, +5 life expectancy).
Spoilers: the Temple's trials

The first trial is a riddle. The answer is "Fire? You are fire." Any other answer wounds you, and the expedition ends.

The second is a demon guardian. Fighting it always wins, and a true mage can open with Power Strike for Combat Magic experience. Running away ends the expedition.

The third is a chalice and two basins, green and red. Drinking either one alone kills you, every time. Drink both and the Horned God speaks to you; kneel or refuse him, and either way you are rewarded. A Shrewd character has a third answer: fill the chalice with water from his own waterskin. It earns the Horned God's favour. Walking away is always allowed.

Once anyone has won the Horned God's favour, later visitors find the temple open and get a shorter, gentler visit.

Mage Summit#

A Mage Summit gathers the world's magic users for a banquet and then a Council of Mages, where a Speaker puts a motion to a vote. With the Order of the Blue Rose off, it is the only way to found the Gnostic Council, and the way its Grand Magus is elected once it exists. With the Order on, which is the default, it does neither: the Order fills the chair.

To host you need the Mage trait, a title above baron, a landed or landless administrative government, and one of three things: a full Secret Knowledge track, a prestige level of 4 or more, or a house with the Magic Traditions modifier, which comes from completing a mythical magic legend. It costs 1,000 gold, halved for each of: Magic Traditions, the 50% discount from the Recognition event, and the Knowledge Sharing Arcana legacy. At its cheapest a player pays 125. Then food: the poor option is free, the default Normal food adds the scaled feast price plus 100 gold, and Good food more again. The cheapest summit, on poor food, is 125 gold. The Extraordinary security option adds 300 gold and nothing in the mod reads it. The cooldown is ten years.

The summit is held at your capital, your domicile, a place of power, or a Mage's Laboratory or Spire. Up to 30 guests: by default every Mage, Yaga, arcane-focus character or Magical Experiments holder in the world, and every True Witch, wherever they are and without any range limit; you can add court mages and learned witches in range. Anyone excluded from the community as an Outcast is left off. A mage who was missed can invite himself with Join the Mage Summit on anyone already going, once a year, and not through the same person twice inside nine years.

There are three intents: Sharing knowledge (the default), Diplomatic affairs (for rulers) and Show superiority (for mages, demons and Yaga). They decide which events find you and which motions you can bring. A member of the Order also has Seek Kindred Minds, which pays standing when another member he knows is there, and a Medicum or higher Speak for My Circle.

If you have the guards check invitations at the opening, one time in three they catch a witch hunter at the door, which costs the host 100 prestige on the spot.

Covens#

A coven is three things at once: a house modifier, a Grand Master at its head, and a culture tradition that gives the coven authority in the realm. The house modifier alone is worth health, fertility and magic experience to every member; the tradition unlocks a cultists regiment, free disinheritance of non-witches, extra benefits on the witch traits, and the Grand Master's own policy intent at the Rite.

You found one with the base game's decision, Found Witch Coven, which Supernatural replaces with an easier one in 2.38: you must be a witch and your house's head, and you need two other adult witches of your house whom you know to be witches, whatever the house's size. The base game wanted four, or three in five adults of a larger house. The AI founds covens too since 2.38, rarely: mostly an ambitious witch, one past fifty, or the head of a house with four or more known witches, never a content one, checked every five years, and never while the world already holds one coven for every 150 counties. The world also makes covens of its own: the Baba Yaga family is one, and a second appears the first time somebody creates the Crown of Shadows. Otherwise you join one by birth or marriage, and rise to lead it at a Grand Rite.

The coven's panel. Every coven shows in the Situations window as Coven, owned by its Grand Master, or by the house head if it has none. It lists the members (every adult of the house the owner knows to be a witch), the coven's reliquary and the decisions below, and is redone each year. Other player members see a copy, and a button on the left of the screen, Your Coven, opens the Situations window. Items pledged to the coven give every member The Coven's Reliquary, up to +15% magic lifestyle experience.

  • Leave the Coven: you are no longer counted among its witches, and what you pledged comes back to you.
  • Disband the Coven: the owner ends it. The house is no longer a coven, and every pledge goes back to its owner.
  • A coven also ends when its house loses a War Against the Witch, and its panel ends when fewer than two adult witches are left in the house.

Covens and the Order. A witch can belong to a coven and to the Order of the Blue Rose at once. A Grand Rite host who is a Magistratus of the Order can choose Bring the Coven to the Order, and every Mage of the coven who could join is sent a note and a rose. A coven may also ask one of its own what he is (The Sisters Ask What You Are): tell them about the Order and lose some standing, or lie and hope they believe you.

Until 2.35 there was no way for an outsider to find out that a house was a coven: nothing let anyone outside the house detect it. Supernatural's contribution is the sweep: when a witch hunt's trail ends at a coven house, a seasoned hunter (Witch-Hunting 60, or an established name as a hunter) can go through the house, up to five rounds in one night, each round another member of that house who is standing in that county. From the second round on he takes a wound before the door even opens, and losing a round ends the sweep the honest way, with him on the floor and four of them still upstairs. If he stops while the culprit is still free, the county records a withdrawal and it costs him 25 prestige. When the culprit belongs to the Order, he can sweep her temple the same way.

The sweep is one night in one house, not a campaign against a dynasty scattered across three kingdoms. House members who are not in the hunted county are never drawn into it.

The Hermetic Order of the Blue Rose#

A secret society of Mages, new in 2.38. It is organised in temples, each under a Temple Magus, and it keeps three words: Silence, Subservience, Observance. Every year each temple chooses a few people with the gift or the mind for it, and the ones who pass its tests are taught to be Mages. The Gnostic Council is its council.

All of it sits behind the Supernatural: The Blue Rose game rule, which is on by default. A save from before 2.38 counts as on. With it off no temple is ever founded, and the Gnostic Council works as Witchcraft ships it. Relics, covens, Zecchia and the Seal of Solomon exist either way.

Membership is not a trait, so nothing on your portrait gives you away. It is a secret, Member of the Hermetic Order, which your Temple Magus learns the day you join. Your temple shows in your Situations as Chapter of the Blue Rose, and a button on the left of the screen, Your Chapter of the Order, opens the Situations window. The Temple Magus owns the temple's panel and every other player member sees a copy of it. Nobody outside the Order sees either.

If the secret is exposed, the county he is in gets a witch trail for hunters to follow, his temple loses that year's Silence reward, and a member who exposed a brother has broken the Order's rules.

Temples#

The Order wants six temples, far apart. At game start a player who is a Mage is offered one in A Temple Without a Master: I will be its Temple Magus founds it at your capital. The rest are seated around the best-placed AI Mages, scored on learning and title rank, with a lean toward Western Europe on the base game's map.

Witchcraft makes no Mages at the start of a game, so on a fresh start there is often nobody to seat. The Order then gathers itself: every year, while fewer than six temples stand, the best-placed AI Mage outside it founds one. That is at most one temple a year, so expect the six to appear over the first years of a campaign rather than on the first day.

  • Size. A temple holds 30 members, 5 more for every 25 years it has stood, up to 60. AI candidates are only called while a temple is under 80% full; the last fifth is kept for players.
  • How many. One temple for every 170 counties in the world. Charters and daughter temples stop there.
  • Petition for a Charter. A landed member who is not a Temple Magus, at standing 75 (60 under More Hands), while someone else sits in the chair, once every ten years. The Grand Magus decides. Granted, you found a temple at your capital and keep your standing.
  • Found a Daughter Chapter. A landed Magistratus who is not the Temple Magus, at standing 75, from a full temple that has not sent out a daughter in fifty years. Up to five AI members who think well enough of you (20 or more) follow you, keeping their ranks.
  • Found the Order Anew. Shown only when no temple stands anywhere. A landed Mage who knows two other Mages who could join (at his court or visiting, his vassals, his liege, or anyone whose witch secret he holds) founds a temple at his capital, and each of them is asked in The Order Again.
  • Succession. A player Temple Magus can Name as Successor in the Temple a Magistratus of his own temple. Otherwise the highest rank, then the highest standing, takes the temple. A player who becomes Temple Magus by any road chooses its way in The Mask of the Temple.
  • A temple with no members left is gone. What its members pledged stays with them.

Once a year each temple sends a note and a rose to up to three people (four under More Hands) inside its Temple Magus's diplomatic range, if it has room. Anyone in one of these groups can be called:

Never called: hunters, demons, golems, necromancers, anyone Bound to the Book, anyone who turned the note down in the last ten years or failed the tests in the last thirty, and anyone who ever left the Order keeping what he knew.

Among those, the temple prefers learning and fit, a member's child (+50), the Source (+30) and a Mage (+20). It favours a young heir to a great title: +25 for an unlanded candidate of 30 or under who is heir to a duchy or more. It passes over AI lords: −30 for a landed AI, and −30 more at kingdom rank, so the Order grows through courtiers rather than kings. A player is never marked down for his lands.

The numbers: the Order's fit

Fit is how well a character would keep the three words. It decides whether a witch or a scholar is in the Order's reach at all, player or AI, and half of it is added to every candidate's place in the Selection. For the AI it also steers whether it answers, which answer it gives at each test and how it behaves as a member. A player chooses his answers freely.

A player's way in#

  • A Name You Have Heard Before. Two scholars arguing over wine, a book merchant, or your confessor. An adult player with learning 12 and a temple in reach has a 20% chance a year, once in a life. Remember the name puts you within the Order's reach whatever your traits and makes the tests easier for ten years. Ask around about it is an intrigue roll; if it works, someone speaks for you.
  • A Rose Among the Things. When a member dies, a player child of his aged 6 or more finds a box with a pressed rose, a rose coin and a page nobody can read. Keep them and the Order can find you for forty years.
  • Seek Out the Order. A decision for a player who is a Mage or has the Source, once every five years, while a temple stands. If a temple in reach has room, you are near the top of its next Selection. If not, a toast says why.
  • Ask About the Blue Rose. A prison interaction on a member whose secret you know. He gives up at most two others of his temple, and has broken the Order's rules. Then you will name me to them lets him go and puts your name forward.

The pledge#

  1. A Note and a Blue Rose. On your pillow, pressed into your hand in the market, or inside the book you were reading. I will come makes you a Neophyte. Not for me keeps you off the list for ten years.
  2. The Old Chapel, a few weeks later. The masks explain the three words and what failing costs. I will take the tests or I will walk out now.
  3. The Coin, a month or two later. Bring back a coin with a rose on it from a tomb, a bell tower, a mill wheel or a cage. Each answer is a roll on one skill: 10 plus 3 for each point of the skill, between 10 and 90, and 15 better if you remembered the rumour. Give my coin to another neophyte always fails: the coin was the test. In the cage, Reach in and grab it costs you a hand if the beast wakes.
  4. The Observance. Someone finds you in an empty house at night and asks what you are doing there. Say nothing passes. Lie well is an intrigue roll. Tell him fails.
  5. Will You Heed the Call? I heed the call makes you an Acolyte with a tutor. A Neophyte at a Conclave takes the oath there, in front of every temple.

Fail at any step and it is Pulveris Memoria: you wake in your own bed, remember nothing of the Order, and cannot be called again for thirty years. Nobody dies in the pledge. A Neophyte still waiting after two years is let go.

Tutors and the first spell#

Every Acolyte is given a tutor, the Medicum or higher of his temple with the fewest acolytes. While he has one he carries Under a Tutor, +10% monthly magic lifestyle experience, and each year there is a small chance the two become friends or rivals.

The teaching takes six years, a year less each for the Source, learning 15 and a Magistratus tutor, and never under three. A player hears The Words in the first year and The Candle That Would Not Go Out in the third. It ends in The First Real Spell: I am a Mage now gives the Mage trait, Witchcraft's own way. A Warlock or True Witch cannot take a second capstone, so she is taught the Order's craft instead (I have learned what the Order can teach me), which counts as taught for the rank of Medicum.

When a player is tutor or acolyte, the pair has a moment now and then: The First Lesson, The Errand, Two Heads Over One Problem (shared Magical Research needs one breakthrough fewer), The Lesson Returned, and once, Late Lessons, which can make them lovers if both are 18 or older, unrelated and fond of each other. A temple also has moments of its own: An Outsider Asks Questions (often a real witch hunter), The Relic That Would Not Sleep, A Neophyte's Test Gone Wrong and The Tutor's Quarrel.

A child born to two members who are both Mages has a 10% chance of the Source (A Spark in the Cradle).

The Conclave#

The masked gathering of every temple, and a new activity. The Order calls one in three cases: when the Grand Magus's chair is empty and three or more temples stand; every ten years, so a Grand Magus who is a member can proclaim the Prerogative, while three or more temples stand; and when a player Grand Magus takes Call a Conclave, at most once in five years. The one asked hosts at a county seat he holds; if he has no land, the Temple Magus of the oldest landed temple hosts for him. It costs the game's usual price for a gathering, and only one Conclave runs at a time.

Every member in the host's reach is invited, acolytes included. A Temple Magus (+40), a council member (+30) and a Neophyte waiting for his oath (+30) are the likeliest to come.

The rules, leaving, and the Old Law#

Breaking the Order's rules marks a member for ten years and costs him 40 opinion with his Temple Magus (Broke Faith). It covers exposing a brother's secret, talking under Ask About the Blue Rose, sending Zecchia after a member you know is one, being caught paying her from the council's funds, a cruel member binding the Vade Maecum, and bending the Old Law. An AI Temple Magus casts out a marked member at the temple's year. No rule of the Order kills.

Leave the Order is a decision any member can take, and being cast out ends the same way. The Order Does Not Let You Walk Away gives two answers:

  • Let them take it: Pulveris Memoria. You forget the Order and lose your magic: the Mage and True Witch traits, the 21 magic perks, your spells, your stored power, the Fors Factorum and any council seat. The Source and the witch trait or secret stay, because you were born with one and other people know the other. You cannot be called for thirty years.
  • I will keep what I know: you keep everything and can never come back. Four times in ten, two to five years later, They Came for What You Know: a learning roll, and if you lose it, Pulveris Memoria anyway.

The Old Law. The Order takes no necromancers. A member who becomes one gets The Forbidden Art each year until it is settled: Renounce it (the Necromancer trait, the Master Necromancer modifier and the right to raise Skeleton Warriors go, with major stress and −20 standing), Let them judge me, or Leave before they cast me out.

Rule One is "with demons, don't". A demon may still Seek a Compact with the Order; the master hears it in A Demon at the Temple Door, and wearing the Seal of Solomon he can make it kneel instead. A demon with a Compact can Borrow from the Reliquary, and owes a favour for every loan.

Hunters and the Order#

  • A hunter who beats a member in a hunt's fight learns his secret and his temple, and takes one of his pledged items as evidence (Tell Me About the Rose).
  • When a witch hunt's culprit is a member, a hunter with Witch-Hunting 60 or a name can choose They answer to a temple and sweep her temple the way he would sweep a coven: up to five rounds, each a member who is in that county. Taking the Temple Magus alive gives him every member's secret.
  • A year later a player hunter gets The Adeptus Major Moves: take 100 gold and forget the house, or refuse and risk Pulveris Memoria on a learning roll. Never a death.
  • Respondeo and Obfuscation Powder are the Order's answer to a trail.

The Gnostic Council#

A Grand Magus and three seats, sitting over the world's magic. Until 2.38 it was Witchcraft's Gnostic Council with an Grand Magus in the chair; 2.38 gave it and its seats the Order's names, and with Supernatural: The Blue Rose on (the default) it belongs to the Order of the Blue Rose.

SeatGives
Grand Magus+3 diplomacy, +50% prestige, double diplomatic range. Appoints the other three, and can Call a secret Gnostic Council meeting every four years once all three seats are filled.
Keeper of the Reliquary+3 learning, +10% lifestyle experience, and Magical Research every five years instead of ten.
Adeptus Major+3 intrigue, +10% scheme discovery, can force a mage duel without losing face, and every Combat Magic project needs one breakthrough fewer.
Chancellor+3 stewardship, +1 influence a month, and Manage of Council Funds every two years.

With the Order on. Only members of the Order sit on the council. Invite to Gnostic Council, Request Gnostic Council appointment and Witchcraft's yearly offer of a seat all refuse anyone else, and leaving the Order, by any road, loses the seat. The chair is filled by the Order: while fewer than three temples stand, the Temple Magus of the oldest temple takes it; with three or more, a Conclave elects the Grand Magus from the Temple Magi, and fills the seats. Taking the chair forms the council if it never existed. A Mage Summit no longer founds the council or elects its chair. Once the Order owns the council, only members see its window, unless the council has revealed itself to the world.

On a save from before 2.38, a Grand Magus who is not a member is dealt with once. A player is asked in The Masks Bow to You to join a temple or give up the chair; an AI who can join is made a Magistratus of a temple, and one who cannot is removed from the chair. A player Grand Magus who is a member can Dissolve the Gnostic Council once every ten years: every seat falls empty, and each holder sent home thinks 15 less of him for ten years.

With the Order off, it is Witchcraft's council under the new names. It does not exist when the game starts, it is founded at a Mage Summit when a Mage Speaker with the Show superiority intent nominates himself and the vote passes, and whenever the chair is empty the next summit is an election.

The Grand Magus also fills seats with Invite to Gnostic Council (a Mage or Yaga; the invitee gains a lot of prestige), and a mage can ask with Request Gnostic Council appointment. He removes a member with Recall from the Gnostic Council, which needs 5,000 prestige, a hook, or an incapable member. Any member can Quit. A seat also qualifies you for the estate's Gnostic Council Archives and its top two tower levels.

The council keeps a treasury. Witches can Make a donation to the Gnostic Council every ten years (50 gold, plus 50 for a count, 100 for a duke, 200 for a king, 250 for an emperor), which makes a non-member a permanent Gnostic Council Supporter (+1 diplomacy). A supporter can Ask for the Gnostic Council's support once the treasury holds 200 gold: three years of protection against plots, research money and +20% magic experience, three years' access to a place of power, a court mage, or help in a war. The Grand Magus decides. With the Order on, members' dues go into the same treasury.

The Vade Maecum Infernal#

A demon's handbook, torn into four pages long ago. Put back together, a Mage can bind himself to it with a Contract: magic without cost and a life that is very hard to end, at the price of his first son. The souls of everyone who failed are still inside it. New in 2.38.

It sits behind Supernatural: The Vade Maecum Infernal: Scattered (the default: three pages start in treasuries), Lost (the three are lost and turn up over the years) or Off (no page is ever made). Under Scattered and Lost the fourth page starts inside Silverback's hide. A save from before 2.38 counts as Scattered.

Finding the pages#

  • Use Magic Triangulation gains Seek the pages of the book, which names whoever holds the book, or else a page.
  • The Necrophone: Where is a page of the book?
  • Send the Golem to Search: your golem is gone two to five years and comes back with a name six times in ten, or wounded and with nothing.
  • Every Champion of the six hears each page that comes within his reach, once: It Wants to Be Whole. Following it costs 5 Hide Bond and tells him who holds it. A Champion who is handed a page gets The Page Calls: guard it (+5 Hide Bond) or let a Mage have it (−5, −15 for Silverback). A Champion holding two pages, or one whose hide holds a page while another is near, is wounded once a year.
  • The book itself, once whole, points its holder at pages, the Seal of Solomon or a rival's relics.

Binding the book, and the Contract#

Bind the Vade Maecum needs a Mage holding all four pages, unsealed, or the whole book, while nobody else has a Contract running. Its tooltip names the son the book will ask for before you start: the eldest living son of your blood, bastards you never knew of included, or for a woman her eldest son. With no such son the book cannot be paid, and the decision says so. The pages become one book, which is protected while the Contract runs (Zecchia and Seize Their Relics leave it alone), and The Book Names Your Son.

You then have three years, shown in your Situations as The Contract:

  1. Name as Witness a courtier, spouse or vassal who agrees. He carries Bound as Witness (+15% stress gain, −20% magic lifestyle experience, −1 learning) until he breaks it with Break the Witness's Bond or the last page is burned. If he dies or leaves you, the step is undone. A council member who thinks well of a man who is not cruel may offer himself (A Councilor Offers).
  2. Give the Finger: A Finger Short, −1 prowess, for life.
  3. Pay the Last Price: your witness alive, the finger given, and your son at your court or in your prison. Pay it and he dies, and you are bound. Give it someone else (another son, or a golem) fails: the book will not be fooled.

Each step works magic that nearby mages feel, and leaves a witch mark on your county. If the time runs out, or the book leaves your hands anyway, or you die before the end, The Book Takes You: your soul goes into it, and it stays whole for its next holder.

Bound to the Book#

The one who pays gets the trait Bound to the Book for life, with Less Than Human (+15 dread, −10 opinion from everyone) and the Arrogant trait unless he is Humble.

  • +4 learning, +25 dread, no death of old age.
  • Magic costs him nothing: every spell and ritual that would spend power is free.
  • Demons obey him. Command your minion works on any demon, greater ones included, and an AI demon always accepts. Summon Demon always offers him the sure option, and Belial goes home instead of turning on him.
  • Murder barely touches him: hostile schemes against him are cut hard in success, ceiling and speed, and he is immune to the strongest curses, domination and mind reading.
  • In battle he is never picked for a knight's or commander's death or wound. Now and then the battle log notes that the blow found the man beside him (The Blow Turned Aside).

Ending him. The sure way is The Unmaking, a hostile interaction for the Grand Magus with two other council members alive and in his reach. It costs him a great deal of power and them a little, and the bound man dies. A hunt fight against him cannot be won without the Enchanted Blade, and even with it he is far stronger unless one of the six hides' Champions came on the hunt. When a bound man dies, by any road, his soul goes into the book.

Who comes for it, and how it ends#

  • While the book exists and no Contract runs, every Champion and every pack alpha in the holder's reach gets a claim on it, and Stinks of the Book (−40) toward him.
  • With the Order on, a bound master who is cruel (sadistic, callous, arbitrary, deceitful or a kinslayer) has every Temple Magus and every council member claiming it, and a cruel member who binds it has broken the Order's rules. An ambitious Grand Magus wants it whoever holds it.
  • The book counts for Seize Their Relics, except while a Contract runs.
  • Tear It Apart (a holder who is not bound, with no Contract running): the book becomes four pages again. Scatter them all, or give one to a Champion in reach, whose hide then keeps it.
  • Burn a Page: a page holder who is a Champion or has one among his courtiers or vassals. When all four are burned the book is gone for good, every witness is freed, and the burner and everyone who promised the voices to free them get They Are Free.
  • Destroyed by any other means, it falls apart into four scattered pages.

Whoever holds the whole book hears it: about one year in three a voice offers learning, a rival's secret, where something is, or the temptation to bind it. A player can read The Names in the Book, the souls it has taken.

The endgame: crises and the two wars#

Witchcraft has an endgame, timed by the Witchcraft: Ending crises rule. At the default, "At any time", the first crisis can come ten years in; the other settings delay it 50 to 200 years, and Never turns it off. After that, each year there is a chance that a crisis starts, if a player has become powerful enough to provoke one:

  • The Grand Coalition: the world's leading witch hunter raises a coalition against a player Mage who rules an independent empire and has drawn too much attention. It never fires while the Witch Hunters Activity rule is on Low, which is the default.
  • Bael: the demon comes for a player mage past 50 Secret Knowledge who holds two magical artifacts, unless the Bael rule is off.
  • Return of the Grand Magus: Abdul Al-Hazred, author of the Black Grimoire, arrives to reclaim the title from a player who holds the Gnostic Council's Grand Magus seat, has 70 Secret Knowledge and two magical artifacts. He demands your artifacts, then sets out to destroy your dynasty: a summoned assassin, a curse on your heir, a double who sits in your council and spends your money, a bounty on your family. He can be stopped. Its rule's "Cannot appear" setting does not block achievements.

There are two casus belli. Demonic Invasion belongs to a Greater Demon with a full Reputation track, costs 2,000 prestige, and on victory takes the titles and every magical artifact, relic and page; a defender who has sold his soul is executed, anyone else goes to the dungeon. Losing it, the demon pays and is imprisoned. War Against the Witch cannot be declared: it comes from the Grand Coalition, or from a witch hunter's Stop The Witch when he chooses to raise a coalition. If the coalition wins, the witch's house loses its coven, every magical artifact, relic and page is taken, the witch is burned, and the witch's adult heir and every adult house member who is not an independent ruler go to the dungeon. A third, Seize Their Relics, came with 2.38: a player can declare it at any distance, and a greedy or ambitious AI Mage against a neighbour.

Becoming one#

Start as one: Minor Demon is in the ruler designer. Otherwise there are three roads. Be born to a demon: the child may carry Demonic Heritage, which turns into the real thing in their twenties. Children#

Demons can have children. That is governed by its own game rule, Supernatural: Demon Inheritance/Pregnancy, separate from the werewolf one. At its default two demons have a 75 per cent chance of a demon-blooded child, and a demon with a human, a werewolf or a hybrid has 50. A Greater Demon parent adds 15 to either. That child is demon-blooded or human, never a wolf. Turn the rule off and demons rely on adoption, which is what the mod did before the rule existed.

Not every pregnancy holds. When either parent is a creature, some pregnancies end when they would start to show. Between two demons that is about seven in ten at first, and more with each demon-blooded child they have, so a demon couple raises a handful of children over a long life, not a dozen.

Summoning#

The door is the Summon Demon decision, which needs the black-magic tree's Summoner perk, enough stored magical power, and eighteen months since the last attempt. It is always a black-magic act.

The ritual can go wrong. The binding botches 30 per cent of the time. For a Mage with 35 or more on the demonology track that falls to 3. A botch gives you a raging demon and a choice: wrestle it, at a one-in-ten chance it kills you outright, or duel it on learning, where a loss is fatal one time in ten. Back out and you lose nothing but the attempt.

What you get, if it works, is rolled. Most of the time it is an unnamed minor demon. The rest of the table is named singletons: Belial, Lilith, Mammon, Samael, Moloch, Abaddon, Hecate, Nyx, Asmodeus, the Blob, Harmonius and Eshu, and each of them is gated on there being no living character already carrying that name. Kill Belial and Belial can be summoned again; leave him alive and his branch is dead weight. Several are player-only, and several need a Mage. Since 2.38 the table also holds Zecchia, a thief who comes back each time she is called. A man Bound to the Book is always offered the sure option.

A botched-but-recovered summoning can only ever produce a minor demon. The named branches all require a clean binding.

The named ones are not equal. Asmodeus is the extreme case: he arrives with a landless empire title, an army of demonic knights, 50,000 gold and five prestige levels. Lilith is the only one with a life of her own. She re-ages herself to eighteen every few years, heals whatever ails her, finds a new lover when her last one dies or leaves, and periodically schemes to murder his spouse. Killing her clears her blessing and puts her back in the summoning table. The chain never ends; it resets.

A failed binding produces a different creature entirely: a demon that escapes, becomes your nemesis, rivals your heir, takes an exile title and starts a murder scheme against you. As of 2.35 it also leaves a mark on the county, which is what lets a hunt come looking for it.

Zecchia#

A minor demon who steals for a fee, new in 2.38. She is one woman, a Minor Demon of House Hellion with the Source, greedy and fond of drink, who comes back each time she is called; if she is ever killed, the next summoning makes another. She moves things, never people or titles.

There are three ways to reach her:

  • Summon Demon. She is one more entry in the roll (8 of 137 when every entry is open), for a clean binding while she is not already working for someone. The first time, A Thief Answers.
  • Study the Black Grimoire can turn up A Name in the Margin: her name.
  • Summon Zecchia, a decision for anyone who knows her name, every two years: a Mage pays a standard spend of power, and a wearer of the Seal of Solomon pays nothing. Her name passes to your heir when you die. A member of the Order who calls her loses 10 standing (Rule One), unless the Order is treating with the Pit.

How Much Will You Take? One to five things; four and five need 400 and 500 gold in hand, and a Chancellor can put it on the Gnostic Council's funds (The Council will pay). She asks a personal price as well: a long drink (major stress and some gold), something of yours (your best ordinary artifact, or 100 prestige), or a lock of your hair. Then you have ninety days to point her at someone with Send Zecchia After Their Treasures.

Ninety days later, Zecchia Delivers: up to the agreed number of the target's items, the best first, pledged and lent ones included. She will not take a hide, the Seal, the book while a Contract runs, a dynasty or house banner, or anything tied to a title. Her fee is paid then: 100 gold for each thing agreed, plus 50 to 200 for each thing she brought, by its rarity. The same hirer cannot set her on the same victim again for ten years. If you never name anyone, Zecchia Is Getting Bored: name them now, pay half and let her go, or ask for more than you agreed, in which case she robs you instead.

A later victim who goes to her while she holds your lock of hair can ask Who hired you against me?, and she tells him. Sending her after a member of your own temple, or one you know is a member, can mark you as having broken the Order's rules.

House Hellion#

Nearly every demon in the game belongs to one house. The dynasty is displayed as Hell and the house as Hellion, two different words for the same family, which is the single most confusing thing about this content. A second house, Maledictio, belongs to the same dynasty and holds one demon. Both carry a white-and-red snake on black.

Sixteen of the seventeen demon templates place their character into House Hellion, which is why the house grows over a long campaign: every summoning, every escaped binding, every demonic courtier adds a member.

Fixed in 2.35 (10 September 2026). The invented-witch template used by witch hunts also placed its character in House Hellion, so every woman a hunt turned up joined the demons' house. A live campaign came back with 246 living members, almost all of them invented witches. Witches are now born without a dynasty. Demons keep the house.

The Demonic lifestyle#

Seventeen perks in one tree with two arms, one for war and one for guile, meeting at a root that hands out +10 to every skill and +10 prowess, the largest flat stat block anywhere in the mod. The war arm ends at Greater Demon; the guile arm ends at Crossroad Dealmaker and the cyrograph.

Two focuses, Manipulation and Destruction, and both carry +30 per cent stress gain. The Demonic lifestyle is the only one in the mod that taxes you for being in it.

It is also designed to be finished and abandoned: taking both capstones clears your focus and closes both focuses to you permanently. And because summoned demons are handed all seventeen perks the moment they appear, the lifestyle is in practice only for a mortal who became a demon. A Prince of Hell has already finished it before you meet him.

Exorcism#

Before 2.35 the mod's only answer to a demon was killing it, and demons are immortal. Upstream's Banish Demon spell does not banish. It kills, and every greater demon is immune to it. Meanwhile a greater demon can recruit another demon into its court every four years, forever. The population compounded and had no drain.

Exorcism is that drain, and it is the hunter's verb rather than the mage's: it costs no magical power at all. It sits inside the demon hunt, as one of three things you can do at the confrontation.

A hunter who later learns to put demons down will refuse to pay when the bill arrives, and the demon has a war that executes soulless defenders outright.

Training one#

Two interactions, and neither costs anything. Train as a Hunter is the offer. Ask to be trained as a Hunter is the mirror, and it is player-only: the AI never asks, and to use it you must already know the other person is a hunter.

The trainee must be at least 13, must not already be partway through someone else's training, and must not have turned you down in the last five years. All three are recent. It used to be possible to train a four-year-old, and pressing the button twice used to queue two copies of the whole chain onto the same person.

Training starts with the specialisation, monsters or witches. It then runs 75 to 115 days before the event that decides whether it took. The base odds are four in five, better for the young, strong and healthy. A failed roll wounds the trainee and leaves them an apprentice for ten years. Nobody dies of training. An AI hunter only ever offers this to someone who already likes them.

An apprentice can be brought on a hunt hosted by their trainer. If that hunt finds its creature or drives it off, they graduate at the door with a head start on the track. If it ends in a withdrawal they do not: there was no lesson in it.

The lifestyle#

Two focuses, Monster Hunter and Witch-Hunter, worth +3 martial and +3 intrigue respectively, and those attribute points are the whole mechanical payload of a focus here. Everything else in a focus is the AI deciding what kind of hunter it wants to be.

Two trees of eight perks each. The Monster Hunter tree has two roots that converge on The Witcher. The perk adds +35 life expectancy and grants the True Monster Hunter trait: +5 prowess, +3 martial and half a prestige a month. Stacked with Built Different's +10 and the trait's own +5, a finished monster hunter lives fifty years longer than he would have.

The Witch-Hunter tree ends at the Witch-Hunter perk, which grants the True Witch Hunter trait. Several of its perks unlock things:

  • Anti-Mage, the root, opens witch trials and the old witch-hunting decision.
  • Combat Spells reaches the Witcher's elixirs, letting a hunter brew potions without being a witch.
  • Protection Runes grants the witch tree's own protective perk, which more than thirty magic gates read. It did nothing at all until it was noticed that the two perks' names differ by one letter.
  • Hunter Academy unlocks the regiment.

A hunter can never take a witch, mage or warlock capstone. The perks refuse to be picked rather than failing silently, which is what they used to do.

Hunter Trainees#

The hunter's regiment. Skirmishers, 50 to a stack and ten sub-regiments at the cap, needing both the Hunter trait and the Hunter Academy perk to raise or reinforce, so an heir can field an inherited regiment but cannot replace its losses until he has earned the perk himself.

They do not out-fight monsters and are not meant to. One vampire sub-regiment hits for 1,850 and takes 1,750; a full trainee stack hits for 700 and takes 900. The counter is the point: a quarter against monsters and animals, and a tenth against demonic knights. A maxed hunter's guard neutralises one monster regiment, not an army of them.

Fielding them is also one of the ways to bring the county's men to the door.

Being known#

Hunters have secrets too. Depending on what your rite, or a liege's above you, holds about hunters, you either carry a hidden Hunter secret or the open Known Hunter trait. The trait is +15 opinion between known hunters and −20 with vampires, werewolves and hybrids, and gives a rite that favours creatures a crime to charge you with.

A rite takes the same four positions on hunters as it does on each kind of creature. What your rite thinks of you has them, and The known traits compares all five.

The trailThe hunt#

The hunt gives each kind of creature a way of leaving evidence in the county it fed in, and builds a three-week activity around a hunter riding there to find out what it is and who it is. Both sides are playable. The creature gets a screen at every point the hunter does.

Trails and what leaves them#

Everything here runs on one thing: a county that carries Something Stalks Here. The modifier is worth −5 county opinion and nothing else, and it does not suppress growth. The cost of a creature living somewhere is the drain each incident does, not a standing penalty.

A county holds four facts under that modifier: who the culprit is, what kind it is, how many incidents there have been, and whether the lord has been told. The kind is shown only to a hunter who has learned to read wounds; everyone else sees a county with a problem.

The first incident is usually only a whisper. It leaves no modifier, no map pin, no culprit and nothing a Trailsman can read, and it expires in a year. A second incident inside that year opens a real trail immediately. A first incident can also be noticed outright on a roll: 30 per cent for a body, 12 per cent for a prowl. Once a trail is open, every later incident escalates it without a roll, up to a cap of six.

A demon does not feed, it ruins, so the county pays for it twice. A witch is the quietest of the four. The county loses faith in its lord faster than it loses people.

What counts as an incident. Killing on a feed, a feed somebody talks about (a witness who got away, a compulsion that did not take, or about one clean feed in ten), a werewolf's border run, a demon escaping a botched binding, a witch's plague spell, the corruption scheme, a body you chose to leave as your own, and a body you pinned on someone else. Roughly two dozen places in the game write a mark. Since 2.36 a feed nobody talks about leaves no mark at all, and the first warning that a county remembers you reads differently when what it remembers is talk rather than a body.

What does not. A death in a tournament. A death in war, or with either party in an army. Killing a prisoner. And any death inside a hunt. A body that turns up inside a supernatural hunt is the hunt's business. Hunters never leave marks, because a hunter is not a creature. Since 2.35a the killer also has to be in the same province as the victim, so a murder scheme carried out from somewhere else no longer counts as a creature's kill. A murder in your own hall still does.

Forgetting. There is no decay event. The modifier is added for five years and refreshed on every incident, so a trail dies five years after the last thing that happened. You can shorten it deliberately: leaving the county clears one mark, paying the lord off clears one and leaves him a debt he will remember if a hunter later proves what was living there, and a compulsion in a county carrying your own trail can bury an incident outright.

And a trail whose culprit has died goes cold: no hunt can ever answer it. When the culprit dies the game looks for someone in that county who could plausibly have been doing it, whether a creature or a witch in the holder's court or the holder himself, and hands the trail to them, re-deriving the kind. If there is nobody, the trail is wiped.

A census on a live 346 AC save, taken before that change, found 23 trails in the world and only four of them warm: nineteen counties carried a mark no hunter could answer, for five years each.

Riding out#

The Supernatural Hunt is an activity. You need the Hunter trait, you must not be recovering from your last one, and there must be something within reach worth hunting. The button greys out and tells you which of those is missing. Since 2.35a a hunt ends if its host is not a hunter.

It costs half what a vanilla activity of its size costs, an eighth for a landless adventurer, and nothing if you are riding under a contract. The cooldown is two years.

There are two kinds. A Monster Hunt needs a county that carries a trail left by a living creature. A Witch Hunt can be held anywhere in your own lands, a vassal's or your liege's, and if you ride it to a county that carries a witch trail with a live culprit, it runs the whole monster spine instead of the thin version.

Where you can ride. A hunt is held at a county's capital, because a trail is a fact about a county. You can reach anywhere within your court's ordinary range, or anywhere at all under your own top liege, so a duke can hunt across the kingdom, a king across the empire. A landless adventurer's reach is five times a landed hunter's radius.

What you are allowed to hunt. A creature that has beaten hunters before is gated by reputation. A legend of 2 or more wants a Monster-Hunter track of 40, or two hunters riding with you. A legend of 4 or more wants a track of 75, or 40 and three hunters. The hunters counted are the ones in your court. A landless hunter, or one with fewer than two trails in his realm, is let through either gate. And a county that has been hunted in the last four years is closed while it catches its breath.

Who stands with the creature#

When a hunt reaches its end in a county, the game walks that county's holder, courtiers, guests and any player standing there, and asks of each one: are you a creature, are you not a demon, are you not going to ground yourself, and do you have a reason to stand. Kin qualifies, meaning anyone linked by claw or by blood, and since 2.37 a wolf of the same pack. So does the defender being a legend, and so does not disliking them. Vampires stand with werewolves under the same rule; kind does not matter at the door.

Only the first three joiners change the fight, and only by a fixed amount: kin are worth 12 each, strangers 6, so three kin take 36 points off the hunter's side. A fourth joins the tally only if the defender is a legend in his own right. For scale, a hunter's kit is worth 15, daylight 15, the county's own men 10, and each hunter who came with him 5.

Going to ground sends word up and down the line. You can warn your get, warn the one who made you, warn all of them, say nothing, or point the hunter at your own progeny, which writes a real mark on their county with their name on it and costs you stress to do. Werewolf kin travel the same machinery as vampire bloodlines here: where the code looks for a progeny and finds none, it falls back to whoever you clawed.

Covering your own kills#

When a player's creature kills someone in person, the next morning brings What They Will Find, and up to four answers:

  • Make it look like nothing. A roll on your strength and your talent for lying. Clean, and there is no mark at all.
  • Find whoever would have had to write this down. No roll, and it costs 40 to 250 gold by the county's wealth. The lord takes your money, the county takes a mark off, and he remembers who paid.
  • Let them look at [name]. Offered when there is someone to frame. The same roll fifteen points harder, and a success writes the mark against another creature carrying their kind, so a vampire who dresses a body as a wolf's work sends the county's next hunter to a wolf's door. You cannot frame your own maker, progeny or the one you clawed.
  • Let them know it was me. No roll. The trail is yours and it is loud: three incidents at once, and every soul in the lord's hall is told what walked through. Hunters will come.

A botched feeding has its own answers, one of which is to silence the witness.

Compulsion works the other way. It is not an incident. Used in a county carrying your own trail it cleans, at 30 per cent with no power behind it and up to 80 at a full track. Used badly, and low power is the main way to be sloppy, it leaves a hole instead. Three holes inside five years and the county picks up Nobody Here Remembers, and its lord starts asking why, which is its own investigation and can end with a real trail carrying your name.

The Trailsman#

A court position, and a camp officer version for landless adventurers. The employer does not have to be a hunter.

The Trailsman walks your counties and reads what has been left in them. Bodies found at the edge of a village, herds taken in a way no wolf takes them, a road nobody will use after dark. He goes and looks, and he comes back and tells you where. A good one comes back with a name.

Summons, bounties and contracts#

On the third incident, the county's lord is told. Something Stalks gives him five answers: look into it himself, put his spymaster on it, post a bounty, send for a hunter, or write it off as superstition and keep bleeding control.

Looking into it is an intrigue duel against how well the creature hides, which scales with its power. Winning reveals the culprit to him by name. The spymaster runs the same duel with a bonus for being a schemer, and a Trailsman turns the compelled-county version into a far better one.

Post a bounty creates a real task contract that any landless adventurer hunter can take off the board: the county's menace, with a purse on it. That needs the landless expansion.

Send for a hunter needs nothing. Three letters go out with names on them, and the lord is shown three men in the order of what they are asking for. The roster is built nearest first: the man in your own hall, then the men who owe you, then the world. The fee starts at 60 and rises with the hunter's Monster-Hunter track, between 40 and 150 in all. It does not depend on the lord's purse. The hunter can accept, refuse, or write back naming a higher price, which doubles it.

A county can only be answered once, because the first lord to write stamps it for ten years, so two lords can never both have a man on the same trail. And a hunter who holds both a bounty contract and a summons for the same county is paid twice, which is deliberate.

Every creature and every hunter lives behind a secret until it is known. What happens then depends on the rite: each rite takes a position on each kind, and that position decides the opinion, the crime and, since 2.36, what your own rite makes of how you live.

Secrets and exposure#

What happens when you turn depends on what your rite thinks of you. The rule is the same for every kind; a vampire is the example here. If your rite, or a liege's above you, shuns or criminalises vampirism, you get a hidden secret, and the people who made you are told about it so your own spymaster stops rediscovering it forever. If nobody around you cares, you get nothing at all, and you go on being a vampire that the game has no public record of.

There is a slow fuse as well, for every immortal. A supernatural who has been immortal more than eighty-five years and is over a hundred and twenty-five rolls three per cent a pulse that somebody finally works out why the lord has not aged.

Membership of the Order of the Blue Rose is a secret of the same kind, Member of the Hermetic Order. Exposed, it marks the county the member is in for witch hunters.

What your rite thinks of you#

Each rite takes one of four positions on each kind of creature, set as a doctrine: Criminal, Shunned, Accepted or Virtuous. Since CK3 1.20 these sit on the rite rather than the faith, so two rites of one faith can disagree, and a new rite gets them too. Criminal is −30 opinion from everyone not of your kind and a legal reason to imprison or execute; Shunned is −15. Under Criminal, since 2.36, a known creature is a sin of that rite as well, which costs it another 20 with the faithful. Virtuous makes Known Vampire a virtue of the rite and costs a great deal of piety to adopt.

Your rite and fulfillment#

Crusader Kings III 1.20 added Spiritual Fulfillment, a measure of how well a character's life fits their faith. Since 2.36 a creature's own rite weighs three of the things it does: a feed the victim lives through, a kill by fang or claw, and turning someone into your own kind. This applies to vampires, werewolves and hybrids alike, each judged by the rite's doctrine for its own kind.

Your rite holds your kindWhat the act does
VirtuousRaises fulfillment: 3 for a feed, 5 for a kill, 10 for a turning. Known or not.
CriminalLowers it by the same amounts, but only once you are known for what you are. A creature behind a secret lives as a member of its faith in good standing and is not charged.
Shunned or AcceptedNothing.

How hard it lands depends on how much you care. Vanilla's own multipliers apply, so a zealous character feels a loss twice over, and a cynic is never charged at all. A known creature whose rite outlaws its kind also cares less about that rite: −25 per cent on every fulfillment gain and loss, −10 per cent if the rite only shuns it. A toast tells you why each time it happens.

Hunters are weighed the other way round. Putting a creature down is righteous where its kind is outlawed and sacrilege where it is revered, 3 either way, whether or not the creature was known: a slaying is the creature unmasked. And since 1.20, choices in the mod's events count through your personality as vanilla's do, so an option that goes against a virtue of your faith costs a little fulfillment, creature or not.

The reliquary#

A temple's or coven's reliquary is everything its members have pledged to it. Pledge to the Reliquary one of your own relics or magical artifacts (not a hide, a page, the book or the Seal); a member of both a temple and a coven chooses which. The item stays yours, and while it is pledged it cannot be taken in a siege or a raid. War, Zecchia and a hunter's evidence can still take it. Take It Back unpledges it.

Every member gets The Reliquary (a temple) or The Coven's Reliquary: +1% monthly magic lifestyle experience for each point, at 2 points for a relic and 3 for a magical artifact. A temple's bonus stops at +30%, a coven's at +15%.

Ask to Borrow lets a Magistratus borrow an item pledged to his own temple, once in five years; an AI lender weighs his opinion and the borrower's rank, and always obeys his own Temple Magus. A loan lasts three years, the lender keeps a claim, and it comes home early if the borrower dies, is cast out or leaves. An item can be lent once in three years.

The Seal of Solomon#

A brass ring cut with the seal of the king who bound demons. It is a holy relic to every Abrahamic faith, and starts enshrined in Jerusalem if an Abrahamic ruler holds it and the shrine has room, else at another Abrahamic holy site that fits, else in the treasury of the greatest Abrahamic AI ruler, else it is lost for 10, 20 or 30 years. Its county carries The Seal of Solomon Is Kept Here (+2 county opinion). A Seal that is destroyed is never made again.

Worn, it stops magical domination. Zecchia cannot be sent after its wearer or rob him, and a wearer who knows her name can summon her without being a Mage or spending power. The Order's master, wearing it, can make a demon kneel. It cannot be pledged, and Zecchia never takes it.

To take it from its shrine: hold the county; or, as a Mage or someone who knows where it is, finish a siege of that county (The Ring Beneath the Altar); or go there on pilgrimage knowing it is there (The Ring at the Shrine: an intrigue roll, 3 for each point plus 10, between 10 and 80; caught, the county's lord thinks 30 less of you). The Necrophone, Zecchia and the book can each tell you where it is.

Seize Their Relics#

A new casus belli. A landed Mage, Order member or coven member (not a landless adventurer) can declare it on anyone holding at least three magical items that can move: magical artifacts, relics, pages, or the book while no Contract runs. Hides never count. A player may declare at any distance; the AI only against neighbours, and only when it is a greedy or ambitious Mage.

Victory takes every relic and magical item the defender owns, pledged or not, and leaves him a claim on each. A white peace costs the attacker a little prestige. Defeat costs him gold and prestige, but nobody loses a claim over it. Witchcraft's Demonic Invasion and War Against the Witch now take relics and pages too.

Other modsCompatibility and what it overrides#

Supernatural runs on the base game and needs nothing else. The compatibility questions that matter come from what it replaces. The mods you must not run beside it are listed under Install and load order, with the load order.

What it replaces#

CK3 resolves mod files by path, last loaded wins. A mod file sitting at the same path as a base-game file replaces that file entirely. Anything the vanilla copy defined and the replacement does not is simply gone. Most of Supernatural is new filenames, which collide with nothing. The exceptions are the places it deliberately steps on vanilla: the birth on-actions, the death on-actions, by key, the base game's Found Witch Coven decision, several character interactions including disinheritance, some scripted relations, travel options, a handful of secret effects, and a set of portrait and accessory gene files. The mod ships its own list of these.

Two mods that both replace the same vanilla file are mutually exclusive in practice. That, rather than anything specific to witches, is why Witchcraft and V&L cannot sit beside this one.

Mid-save#

Adding it to a running campaign works, and every past release has been safe to add to a save. It is built for it in places: existing saves get a one-time repair pass for secrets nobody was told about, creatures that predate the disease-immunity flag are retro-fitted on a yearly pulse, characters stuck mid-transition are released, and the hunt's availability check is written to fail open on a continued save rather than grey the activity out forever.

Two things do not carry over. Game rules are chosen when a save is created, so a campaign started without Supernatural has no values for its twenty-six rules. The ones added in 2.37 and 2.38 are read so that a save from before them gets their defaults: the hides scattered, packs on, the Order on and the Vade Maecum scattered. On such a save the Order seats its temples, and the relics, the Seal of Solomon and the pages are placed within the first year. And the mod adds portrait and accessory genes, which is the usual CK3 hazard around adding or removing a mod mid-save.

Removing it from a save is not supported and there is no uninstall path. Most of what strands is mod-created and disappears with the mod. The exception worth knowing: the mod sets its four creature doctrines on every rite in the save, at game start, whenever a rite is created and once a year, and those are vanilla rite objects that persist. Nothing takes them off again.

Multiplayer#

Every player needs the same mods at the same positions. The mod honours CK3's own multiplayer murder-scheme protections in its own scheme, and carries no other multiplayer-specific code.

When something looks wrongCommon questions and troubleshooting#

Short answers for when the game does something you did not expect. How each system works is in its own section; Quick answers covers the questions asked most.

The launcher does not show the mod after a manual install.

Check that both supernatural_mod.mod and the supernatural_mod folder are in the CK3 mod folder, and that the .mod file's path= line is not pointing at a folder on another machine. Install and load order has the detail.

Where are the decisions to become a vampire or a werewolf?

Behind a game rule that is off by default. Supernatural: Embrace Decisions ships as "Default (Off)". It gates eight decisions: four to become a vampire, werewolf, hybrid or hunter, and four to stop being one, three of which are the cures. Turn it on at game setup if you want to pick what you are. The eight decisions by name.

Why is there no temple of the Order in my game?

The Order seats its temples around AI Mages, and Witchcraft makes none at the start of a game. Until a Mage appears, there is nobody to seat. Once there is, the Order founds at most one temple a year until six stand, so on a new campaign expect them over the first years. With no temple anywhere, a landed Mage who knows two others can use Found the Order Anew. Check also that Supernatural: The Blue Rose is on. Temples.

Why can I not start Magical Research?

It needs a true Mage, meaning the trait without the False Mage secret, an adult, holding at least a county, off its ten-year cooldown (five as the Gnostic Council's Keeper of the Reliquary). A Research Expedition also needs a place of power you know about and have not visited. Magical Research.

The Arcane focus is greyed out. What am I missing?

One key on top of being a witch: a magic artifact (the Enchanted Ring and an awakened Singularity count), the Source, the Magical Experiments perk, a place of power held while landed, a Gnostic Council grant, Research Cooperation from a summit, a house with the academy, an event that opens it, or, as a True Witch, an academy standing anywhere in the world. The magic trees.

Do vampires have fangs?

No. The mod has no fangs portrait and no fangs game rule. If you have a fangs option in your barbershop, it belongs to another mod. How a vampire looks.

Why will a hide not take me?

Because of your traits. It Will Not Have You says which, and that hide will not try you again for five years. What each hide looks for.

I have played for decades and never seen a hunt. Why?

Two things, and 2.35 fixed the larger one. Before it, the AI checked the feeding and hunting decisions on an interval of zero, which in CK3 means never, so almost nothing wrote trails and there was nothing to hunt. The second is structural and still true: a hunt needs a warm trail, meaning the culprit is still alive. Since 2.35 a dead culprit's trail is handed to another creature in the county where there is one, instead of sitting there unhuntable.

Why did a witch from my dynasty disappear?

A bug in 2.35, fixed in 2.35a. Witch Spotted looks for a real witch before it makes one up, and she can be anyone in reach: the culprit of a witch trail, someone at the ruler's own court or the local lord's, the lord himself, someone at the liege's court, or a witch whose secret the ruler knows. The two options that turn the hunt down still carried a clean-up meant only for a made-up woman, so they killed the real one. That is why witches married off into other courts were vanishing. Since 2.35a, turning the hunt down leaves her alone. Anyone lost before the fix stays lost.

Why is my world full of new hunters and werewolves?

Also 2.35, fixed in 2.35a. Two yearly events, Witch Spotted and Monster Sighting, were made far more frequent so hunters would hear of work, and the faster rate reached every ruler on the map, not only hunters. Burning the witch or killing the creature makes a hunter of anyone who was not one, and Monster Sighting can leave a new werewolf in the world, so the map filled up. Since 2.35a only hunters get the faster rate; everyone else is back to the rate from before 2.35. Hunters and werewolves already made stay in your save.

HistoryVersion history#

The mod shows its own changelog once per save, at game start or on the next 1 January after an update. This is what it says, condensed.

2.38 — The Hermetic Order of the Blue Rose#

The Hermetic Order of the Blue Rose comes to Supernatural: temples of Mages that send a note and a blue rose to the people they want, and teach magic to the ones who pass. Members rise from Acolyte to Temple Magus, keep their temple's way of Silence, Subservience or Observance, work toward the Order's Prerogative, and meet in masks at the Conclave, which elects the Grand Magus of the Gnostic Council. Seven lesser relics and the Seal of Solomon join the game; most can be pledged to a temple's or coven's reliquary and lent out, and Seize Their Relics is a war for a rival's hoard. The Vade Maecum Infernal is four scattered pages, one inside Silverback's hide. Zecchia, a demon thief, answers some summonings. Covens have their own panel, can be founded with three witches, and can bring their Mages into the Order. Friendly spells and the council's interactions now sit in the Supernatural menu. Two new game rules turn the Order and the Vade Maecum on or off.

Balance: one capstone each, Mage, Warlock or True Witch; the AI's nature weighs on its magic path and on necromancy; Witchcraft's magical artifacts can reach courtiers and landless adventurers; the werewolf and hybrid traits give less Martial, and the werewolf's ranks are Fledgling Werewolf, Werewolf, Seasoned Werewolf, Dread Werewolf and Elder Werewolf; hide Champions who practise magic regain power every year; packs invite lone wolves they know, and a second wolf at court lowers the bar for founding a pack to Pack Standing 25; True Witches appear again beside the Order's Mages.

Fixes: healing no longer takes away the Horned God's Claws; renouncing necromancy takes away the right to raise Skeleton Warriors; Demand Submission converts its target properly; Shadow Step out of prison runs the base game's escape; a soul-sold character's death pays the cyrograph's owner; creature lifestyle experience shows in tooltips; thousands of script errors are gone.

2.37 — The Six Hides#

Six ancient wolf hides are loose in the world: Silverback, Timber, Tundra, Graybeard, Midnight and Alpha. Each chooses a werewolf or hybrid who fits it, and its Champion is stronger, lives longer, never grows frail and hears magic. Most start as relics in shrines at holy sites; take one by holding the county, by siege or on pilgrimage, or pay someone to fetch it with Send for a Hide. Wolves can found and join packs, rise in Pack Standing, Challenge the alpha, and meet at the new Werewolf Gathering. AI wolves challenge an alpha only for a reason: he is too weak to lead, cruel to his pack, or their enemy. A hide an AI leaves idle for ten years slips away to find a wolf. Two new game rules control the hides and the packs. It works on a running save: the hides turn up at the next new year.

Fixes: the mod's death reasons read as proper sentences; the death of a vampire who refused to feed no longer shows an error; They Stopped Coming has its icon; a broken link in Invoke the Bond is fixed; he, she and his are capitalised at the start of a sentence; a demon can have a child with a werewolf or hybrid.

2.36 — Crusader Kings III 1.20#

Updated for CK3 1.20, which rebuilt religion around rites. On 1.20, 2.35a crashed as a new game loaded in; 2.36 fixes that. The vampirism, lycanthropy, hybrid and hunter doctrines belong to rites now, so every rite of a faith has them and new rites get them too, and crimes, shunning and the witch war follow the rite. Known vampires, werewolves, hybrids and hunters are sins under a rite that outlaws their kind and virtues under one that reveres it, the way 1.20 treats witches. Your own rite judges your feeding, killing and turning others through Spiritual Fulfillment, but only condemns them once you are known; the zealous feel it more and a cynic is never charged. A feed only leaves a trail when somebody talks. The Seekers camp purpose works again, and Scholar is Erudite in 1.20 and keeps the magic bonus.

Fixes: the first warning that a county remembers you no longer mentions a body when there wasn't one; feeding events no longer flood the error log; the Mikaelsons no longer log hundreds of portrait errors at load; French, German, Spanish, Korean, Polish and Chinese players see English rather than raw keys for everything since the hunt. If you play with A Game of Thrones, AGOT has not been updated for 1.20 yet, and the compatibility patch follows AGOT's update.

2.35a — The witch you let live#

Fixes for The Hunt, safe on a running save. Turning down a witch hunt no longer kills the witch, so witches married into other courts stop vanishing. The witch and monster sightings are fast for hunters only and back to their old rate for everyone else, which stops the world filling with new hunters and werewolves. Monster Sighting now has the same conditions as its twin, so it no longer reaches someone imprisoned, incapable, travelling or busy with an activity. An ordinary murder no longer reads as a creature's kill unless the killer was there; a murder in your own hall still can. Characters already in House Hellion stay there.

2.35 — The Hunt#

A new activity: ride out and hunt what is killing people. Kills and prowling leave trails on the counties they happen in, and trails escalate: three incidents and the lord hears about it. He can look into it himself, set his spymaster on it, post a bounty, or send for a named hunter and pay him when it is done. A new court position, the Trailsman, reads the countryside. You can hunt a creature or hunt a witch, each with their own trails and beats. Bring silver, stakes, dogs or men, and the door goes differently. Take an apprentice along and he graduates in the field.

These notes follow the mod's in-game text, which has two slips: there is no dog kit, and the perk is Hunter Academy. Of the creature's answers below, "compel him" is Look them in the eye.

The creature gets the other half of the scene, and real answers: stand and fight, go to ground, compel him, send your progeny, let the wolf out, or talk him onto somebody worse. Get away and you can warn your sire and your get, or sell them. Demons argue instead of running, and may offer a bargain. Hunters and creatures earn names the world starts using. A county that turns three hunters away stops attracting any. You can cover your own kills: pay it off, frame someone, or hide the body. Compel a whole town and the holes start to line up. Hunters ride at bad odds far less often, and can turn back.

Fixes: witch hunts were impossible to reach and now work; two files never loaded, so no trail was ever written; the hunter migration layer never ran; hunters are told about witches and monsters far more often; secret witches leave trails, not only openly known ones; compelling someone for their secrets no longer errors; thousands of script errors a session removed; hunter regiments now need the Hunter Academies perk.
